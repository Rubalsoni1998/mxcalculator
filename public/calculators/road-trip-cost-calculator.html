About the Road Trip Cost Calculator

A road trip is often cheaper than flying for a group, but only if you count every cost: fuel or charging, hotels, food, tolls, parking and the wear you put on your car. This calculator totals all of them, then splits the bill per traveler so everyone knows their share before you leave.

It supports gasoline, diesel and electric vehicles, estimates your drive time and fuel needed, and compares your trip with two published cost-per-mile benchmarks: AAA's full cost of owning a car and the IRS business mileage rate.

How the Calculation Works

Total miles = One-way distance × 1 (one way) or 2 (round trip)
Fuel used = Total miles ÷ MPG (or Total miles × kWh per 100 ÷ 100 for electric)
Fuel cost = Fuel used × Price per gallon (or per kWh)
Lodging = Hotel nights × Price per night
Food = Travelers × Trip days × Food per person per day
Total = Fuel + Lodging + Food + Tolls + Parking + Other + Optional wear
Cost per person = Total ÷ Travelers
Drive time = Total miles ÷ Average speed

Example: A round trip with 600 miles each way is 1,200 miles. At 28 mpg and $3.91 a gallon, you burn 42.9 gallons for about $168 of fuel.

Add two hotel nights at $140 ($280), food for two people over three days at $50 each ($300), $40 in tolls, $30 for parking and $100 for activities. The total is $918, or $459 per person.

Key Terms

  • MPG: Miles per gallon. Your real-world highway number is often different from the sticker, especially with luggage, roof racks, hills or headwinds.
  • kWh per 100 miles: How electric vehicles show efficiency. A lower number is better. Fast chargers on a road trip usually cost more per kWh than charging at home.
  • Wear and tear: Extra maintenance, tires and repairs caused by the miles you add. It is a real cost even though you pay it later.
  • IRS standard mileage rate: A set cents-per-mile amount that businesses can use to reimburse or deduct vehicle costs for business driving. It is not for personal trips, but it is a handy yardstick for splitting costs with others.

Tips to Lower Your Road Trip Cost

  • Share the ride. Fuel and lodging costs stay almost the same with more travelers, so the per-person cost drops quickly.
  • Drive the speed limit. Fuel economy falls as speeds rise above about 50 mph, according to the U.S. Department of Energy, so slower cruising saves money.
  • Check tire pressure and lighten the load. Under-inflated tires and extra weight reduce MPG.
  • Fill up where gas is cheaper. Prices vary by state and often drop away from interstate exits.
  • Plan stays and food. Hotels with free breakfast and a cooler with lunch supplies cut daily costs.
  • Budget for surprises. Add 10% to the total for detours, road closures and unplanned stops.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the cost of a road trip?

Divide your total miles by your car’s miles per gallon to get gallons used, then multiply by the price per gallon. Add hotels, food, tolls, parking and other expenses. The calculator does all of this and splits the total per person.

How much does gas cost per mile?

Divide the gas price by your MPG. At $3.91 a gallon and 28 mpg, gas costs about 14 cents a mile. AAA’s 2026 study puts the average fuel cost for new vehicles at about 17.3 cents per mile.

What is the average cost to drive a car per mile?

AAA’s 2026 Your Driving Costs study puts the average cost to own and operate a new vehicle at $12,863 a year, or about 85.8 cents per mile at 15,000 miles, including depreciation, insurance, financing, fuel and maintenance. Only fuel, tires and maintenance rise with each extra mile.

How do I figure out how much gas I need for a road trip?

Divide the total trip miles by your MPG. A 1,200-mile round trip at 28 mpg needs about 43 gallons. Add a small cushion for traffic, detours and hilly roads.

How much does it cost to charge an electric car on a road trip?

Multiply miles by your kWh per 100 miles, divide by 100, then multiply by the price per kWh. AAA reported public charging near 42 cents per kWh in November 2026, much higher than the roughly 18 cents per kWh used for home charging.

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?

The IRS business rate rose from 72.5 cents to 76 cents per mile on July 1, 2026. The medical and qualifying moving rate is 23.5 cents, and the charitable rate stays at 14 cents. These rates are for deductions and reimbursements, not for personal trips.

How can I split road trip costs fairly?

Total the shared costs (fuel, tolls, parking and lodging) and divide by the number of travelers. Some groups also add a per-mile payment to the driver for wear on the car. Use the cost-per-mile reference table for a starting point.

How many hours will my drive take?

Divide total miles by your average speed. A 600-mile one-way trip at 60 mph takes about 10 hours of driving, plus time for fuel, meals and rest stops. Plan a break every two hours or so.
