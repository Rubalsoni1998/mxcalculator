About the Calories Burned Calculator

The calories you burn during exercise depend on how intense the activity is, how long you do it and how much you weigh. Heavier people burn more energy for the same activity.

This calculator uses MET values, a standard measure of exercise intensity, to estimate calories burned for a wide range of activities.

How to Use the Calories Burned Calculator

  1. Enter your body weight.
  2. Select an activity and enter the duration.
  3. Click Calculate to see the estimated calories burned.

Calories Burned Formula

Calories burned = MET × weight (kg) × time (hours). One MET is the energy you use sitting at rest, so jogging at about 7 METs burns roughly seven times resting energy.

Example: A 70 kg person cycling at a moderate pace (about 8 METs) for 30 minutes burns 8 × 70 × 0.5 = 280 calories.

Typical MET Values

  • Walking at a moderate pace: about 3.5 METs
  • Running at 8 km/h (5 mph): about 8 METs
  • Cycling at a moderate pace: about 8 METs
  • Swimming laps at a moderate pace: about 6 METs
  • Strength training: about 3.5 to 6 METs

Worked Example: 170 lb, 30 Minutes

The formula is calories per minute = MET × 3.5 × body weight in kg ÷ 200. For a 170 lb person (about 77.1 kg) over 30 minutes, using commonly published MET values:

Why Your Watch May Disagree

MET-based estimates use averages. Fitness level, terrain, wind, heart rate and technique all change the real number, and devices differ in how they estimate it. Treat any figure as a rough guide, not a precise count.

Using the Result

  • Heavier people burn more for the same activity, so the figure rises with weight.
  • Do not treat exercise as a license to eat large amounts; the calories burned are often smaller than expected.
  • Combine this with your TDEE to see the larger picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a calories burned estimate?

It is an estimate. Fitness level, body composition, terrain and efficiency can shift the real number by 10 to 20 percent.

Which exercise burns the most calories?

Running, fast cycling, rowing and swimming burn the most per hour, but the best choice is the one you can do consistently.

Do I burn calories after exercise?

Yes, a modest amount, but it is small compared with the calories used during the workout.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

It depends on your goal. If you are cutting weight, eat back only part of what you burn, because estimates can be generous.

What is a MET?

A MET, or metabolic equivalent, compares the energy cost of an activity with resting. One MET is the energy used sitting quietly, so a 4 MET activity uses about four times as much.

Which burns more calories, running or walking?

Running burns more per minute at the same body weight. Walking can still add up, since people can sustain it for longer.

Does body weight change calories burned?

Yes. A heavier person uses more energy for the same movement, so the same workout burns more calories.
