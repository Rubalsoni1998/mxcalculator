About the Closing Cost Calculator

Closing costs are the fees and prepaid expenses due when you finish buying a home, on top of your down payment. They include lender charges, appraisal and inspection fees, title insurance, settlement or attorney fees, government recording and transfer taxes, and prepaid items such as insurance and property tax. This calculator estimates each category and gives you total closing costs and the cash to close you should plan for. Updated November 21, 2026.

How Much Are Closing Costs?

Estimates vary a lot by source because they count different things. Lenders and agents often quote a rule of thumb of 2% to 5% of the purchase price for a buyer, and some quote 3% to 6% of the loan. Government and title data from LodeStar's 2026 report put the national average for title, settlement and government charges alone near 1% of the sale price, with high-transfer-tax states costing much more. Lender fees, points and prepaid items sit on top of that, which is how the totals reach the 2% to 5% range.

What Closing Costs Include

  • Lender fees: Origination, underwriting and processing, plus discount points if you buy down your rate.
  • Third-party fees: Appraisal, credit report, home inspection, survey and flood certification.
  • Title and settlement: Title search, lender's title insurance, escrow or attorney fees.
  • Government charges: Recording fees and state or local transfer taxes.
  • Prepaids and escrow: The first year of homeowners insurance, a few months of property tax and interest from closing day to month end.
  • Upfront mortgage insurance: FHA charges an upfront premium of 1.75% of the base loan. VA loans charge a funding fee of 2.15%, 1.5% or 1.25% for first use depending on your down payment (5%, 5%–10%, 10% or more). Both can usually be financed into the loan.

Example: $400,000 home, 10% down, conventional loan at 7.28%.

The loan is $360,000. Lender origination at 1% is $3,600. Appraisal and other third-party fees are $1,500. Title and government charges at 1.0% are $4,000. Prepaids are $2,000 insurance, about $1,000 of tax escrow for three months and about $1,077 of interest for 15 days. Total closing costs come to about $13,200 (3.3% of the price), and cash to close is that plus the $40,000 down payment.

How to Lower Your Closing Costs

  • Compare Loan Estimates from at least three lenders. Origination fees and title charges are often negotiable or shoppable.
  • Ask for seller credits. In a slower market sellers may pay part of your costs.
  • Close near the end of the month to cut prepaid interest.
  • Skip discount points if you will not keep the loan long enough to recover their cost.
  • Look for down payment and closing cost assistance from state housing agencies and some lenders.

Sources: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey (30-year fixed rate 7.28% as of November 19, 2026), LodeStar Software Solutions closing cost data reports as published by Bankrate (November 2026) and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are closing costs?

Closing costs are the fees, taxes and prepaid items due at the end of a home purchase. They are separate from your down payment and include lender fees, title and settlement charges, government recording and transfer taxes, and prepaid insurance, property tax and interest.

How much are closing costs for a buyer?

A common rule of thumb is 2% to 5% of the purchase price. Actual costs depend on your lender, loan type and state. High transfer-tax states such as Delaware, New York and Vermont cost much more than the national average.

Who pays closing costs, the buyer or the seller?

Buyers usually pay most closing costs. Sellers typically pay agent commissions and, in many states, transfer taxes. You can negotiate for the seller to cover some of your costs as a credit.

Are closing costs included in my down payment?

No. Your down payment goes toward the price of the home. Closing costs are paid separately, so your cash to close is the down payment plus closing costs, minus any seller credit.

Can I roll closing costs into my loan?

Only some of them. FHA and VA upfront fees are commonly financed. For other costs, lenders may offer a no-closing-cost loan that charges a higher interest rate or builds the fees into the balance.

When do I find out my exact closing costs?

Your lender must send a Loan Estimate within three business days of your application and a Closing Disclosure at least three days before closing. Compare them line by line.
