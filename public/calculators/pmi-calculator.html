About the PMI Calculator

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is a monthly cost that lenders add to a conventional mortgage when the borrower puts down less than 20% of the home price. It protects the lender, not you, if the loan goes unpaid. This calculator estimates your monthly PMI, shows how it changes your total monthly payment, and tells you roughly when it can be removed.

Enter the home price, your down payment, interest rate, loan term and credit score range. The tool works out your loan-to-value ratio (LTV), picks an estimated PMI rate for your credit and LTV, and then walks through your amortization schedule to find the month your balance falls to 80% and 78% of the original home value. You can also type in a PMI rate from a real lender quote to see exact numbers.

How PMI Is Calculated

Most borrower-paid PMI is billed monthly as a small percentage of the original loan amount each year.

Loan Amount = Home Price − Down Payment
LTV = Loan Amount ÷ Home Price × 100
Monthly PMI = Loan Amount × Annual PMI Rate ÷ 12
Monthly P&I = Loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)  where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments.

Example: You buy a $350,000 home with 10% down ($35,000). The loan is $315,000 and the LTV is 90%.

With a 700–719 credit score the estimated PMI rate is about 0.50% a year, so monthly PMI is $315,000 × 0.50% ÷ 12 ≈ $131. At 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest is about $1,991, for a combined $2,122 a month before taxes and insurance.

What Drives Your PMI Rate

  • Credit score: This is the biggest factor. A borrower with a 760+ score can pay less than a third of what a 620–639 borrower pays.
  • Loan-to-value ratio: The smaller your down payment, the higher the rate. Rates step up at 85%, 90%, 95% and 97% LTV.
  • Loan term: Loans of 20 years or less usually carry lower PMI because the balance falls faster. This calculator applies a 25% reduction for terms of 20 years or fewer.
  • Occupancy and loan type: Primary homes cost less to insure than second homes and investment properties. Fixed-rate loans are usually cheaper than adjustable-rate loans.

How and When PMI Ends

Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, PMI on most conventional loans is removed in two ways:

  • You request cancellation once your loan balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, provided you are current on payments and have no second liens.
  • Automatic termination happens when the balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the original value, as long as you are current.

The calculator reports both dates based on your regular payment schedule. Paying extra principal brings them forward. If your home has gained value, some lenders also allow cancellation based on a new appraisal.

Ways to Avoid or Reduce PMI

  • Put 20% down. The results table shows exactly how much more you would need.
  • Improve your credit score before applying. Moving up one tier can cut PMI noticeably.
  • Ask about lender-paid PMI. The lender covers the insurance in exchange for a higher interest rate, which cannot be cancelled later.
  • Consider a piggyback (80/10/10) loan or a VA loan, which has no monthly mortgage insurance for eligible veterans.
  • Make extra principal payments to reach 80% LTV sooner.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PMI and why do I have to pay it?

PMI stands for private mortgage insurance. Lenders require it on conventional loans with less than 20% down because a smaller down payment means more risk for them. The insurance repays the lender if you default. It does not protect you.

How much is PMI per month?

PMI typically costs about 0.2% to 1.75% of the loan amount per year, depending on your credit score and down payment. On a $300,000 loan that is roughly $50 to $440 a month.

Is PMI tax deductible?

Mortgage insurance premiums were deductible only in certain past years and under income limits. Tax rules change, so check current IRS guidance or ask a tax professional before counting on a deduction.

When can I stop paying PMI?

You can ask your servicer to cancel once your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value. It must end automatically when the balance is scheduled to hit 78%, if you are current on payments.

Does PMI apply to FHA, VA or USDA loans?

No. FHA loans charge a mortgage insurance premium (MIP), USDA loans charge a guarantee fee, and VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance. This calculator covers conventional loan PMI only.

Is it better to pay PMI or wait until I have 20% down?

It depends on home price trends, rent costs and how long it would take to save. Many buyers choose to pay PMI for a few years rather than wait, since PMI is temporary. Compare the total PMI cost shown here with the cost of waiting.
