About the Investment Calculator

Investing a lump sum and adding to it regularly lets compound growth do much of the work. This calculator shows how much an investment could grow over time at an assumed annual return.

Enter a starting amount, contribution schedule and expected return to see the future value, total contributions and growth.

How to Use the Investment Calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount, number of years and expected return rate.
  2. Choose compounding frequency and enter regular contributions.
  3. Click Calculate to see your projected balance.

Future Value Formula

FV = P × (1 + r)ⁿ + PMT × ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1) ÷ r where r is the rate per period and n is the number of periods.

Example: $10,000 invested with $500 added monthly at 7 percent, compounded monthly for 20 years, grows to roughly $300,800, of which $130,000 is money you put in.

Tips for Realistic Projections

  • Use a conservative return such as 5 to 7 percent for diversified stock portfolios.
  • Remember that returns vary year to year.
  • Check results in today's dollars by accounting for inflation.

Worked Example: $10,000 Plus $500 a Month

Start with $10,000 and add $500 every month for 20 years, with returns compounded monthly. Total contributions are $130,000. The ending balance depends heavily on the return you assume:

These are illustrations, not forecasts. Real returns vary from year to year and are never guaranteed.

Adjusting for Inflation

At a 7% nominal return and 3% inflation, the real return is about 3.9%, and the same plan is worth roughly $202,721 in today's dollars. Use the inflation field in the calculator to see this directly, or read about the effect on the inflation calculator page.

What Moves the Result Most

  • Time. Starting earlier does more than saving more later, because growth compounds on earlier growth.
  • Contributions. Regular deposits are the part you control.
  • Fees. A fund charging 1% a year takes a meaningful share of long-term growth.
  • Taxes. Taxable accounts, 401(k)s and IRAs are taxed differently. See the capital gains tax calculator for taxable accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What return rate should I assume?

Long-term stock market averages are often cited near 7 percent after inflation, but actual returns vary widely.

Is it better to invest a lump sum or monthly?

Lump sums have more time to grow; monthly investing spreads out risk and fits a budget.

How does inflation affect my results?

It reduces buying power. Use the inflation-adjusted figure to see future money in today's dollars.

Does this guarantee my balance?

No. It is a projection, not a promise.

How much will $500 a month grow to?

With monthly compounding at 7% for 20 years and no starting balance, $500 a month grows to about $260,463. Change the return, years and starting amount in the calculator to see your own figure.

What return should I assume?

Use a range rather than one number, for example 4%, 6% and 8%. Long-term returns depend on what you hold, and past results do not guarantee future ones.

Is the result before or after tax?

It is before tax unless you account for it. Taxes depend on the account type and your bracket, so treat the result as a pre-tax estimate.
