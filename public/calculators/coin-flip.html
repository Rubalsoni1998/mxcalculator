About the Coin Flip

This is a fair online coin flip for settling a decision, running a quick experiment or teaching probability. Flip one coin or up to 10,000 at a time, keep a running count of heads and tails, track your calls, and see how your results compare with the 50/50 you would expect. The result of each flip is chosen with your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator.

How It Works

Each flip = random bit from crypto.getRandomValues (0 = tails, 1 = heads)
Percent heads = Heads ÷ Total flips × 100
Expected heads = Total flips ÷ 2, with standard deviation √n ÷ 2
z-score = (Heads − n ÷ 2) ÷ (√n ÷ 2)

Example: In 100 flips you expect about 50 heads, with a typical swing of about 5 either way. Getting 60 heads is a z-score of (60 − 50) ÷ 5 = 2, which happens by chance only about 4.6% of the time. Getting 55 heads (z = 1) is quite ordinary, since results that far from 50 happen about 32% of the time.

Are Coin Flips Really 50/50?

A physical coin flip is governed by physics, not pure chance. A 2007 paper by Persi Diaconis, Susan Holmes and Richard Montgomery showed that a flipped coin tends to land on the same side it started, with a probability of about 51%. In 2023 a team led by František Bartoš collected 350,757 hand flips and found the coin landed on the same side 50.8% of the time, almost exactly as predicted, while heads and tails were equally likely overall. The effect shrinks with practice and is tiny, so it only matters if someone knows the starting side and bets many times. If a flip matters, keep the starting side hidden.

A digital flip does not have this problem. There is no starting side, and the browser's random number generator picks heads or tails with equal probability. It is not a physical measurement of a real coin, so a result here is as fair as any coin toss can be.

Key Terms

  • Fair coin: A coin with equal chance of heads and tails.
  • Law of large numbers: With more flips, the percent of heads gets closer to 50%, although the gap between counts can still grow.
  • Gambler's fallacy: The mistaken belief that a coin is "due" for tails after a run of heads. Each flip is independent.
  • Streak: A run of the same result in a row. Streaks are more common than most people expect.

Tips for Using Coin Flips

  • Call before you flip. Pick heads or tails first so no one can change the call afterward.
  • Use it for tiebreakers. A fair flip is a quick, neutral way to break a tie.
  • Notice your reaction. If you feel disappointed by the result, that is a clue to what you actually wanted.
  • Do not expect balance. Short runs can be far from 50/50 and still be perfectly random.
  • For important decisions, use a coin flip only as a tiebreaker after weighing the facts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this coin flip really random?

Yes. Each flip uses your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator, which gives heads and tails equal probability.

Is a real coin flip 50/50?

Almost. Research shows a hand-flipped coin lands on the same side it started about 50.8% of the time, so the effect is very small. Heads and tails are equally likely when the starting side is random.

Can I flip many coins at once?

Yes. Choose up to 10,000 flips and the calculator shows the counts, percentages and longest streak instantly.

Why do I get long streaks of heads or tails?

Streaks are normal in random data. In 100 flips, a run of 5 or more of the same side is quite likely.

What is the chance of 5 heads in a row?

A run of 5 heads in 5 flips has a 1 in 32 chance, which is 3.125%.

What is the gambler's fallacy?

It is the belief that after several heads in a row, tails is more likely. Each flip is independent, so the chance stays 50% every time.

Is my result saved?

No. Counts are only kept while the page is open, and nothing is sent to a server.

How unusual is my result?

The calculator shows a z-score and the chance of a result at least this far from 50/50 happening by luck, once you have 20 or more flips.
