About the Standard Deviation Calculator

Standard deviation measures how spread out a set of numbers is around its mean. A small value means the numbers cluster tightly; a large value means they are widely scattered.

Enter your data and the calculator returns the mean, variance, and both sample and population standard deviation.

How to Use the Standard Deviation Calculator

  1. Enter your numbers separated by commas or spaces.
  2. Choose sample or population.
  3. Click Calculate to see the results.

Standard Deviation Formula

Population: σ = √(Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N). Sample: s = √(Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1)).

Example: For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the mean is 5 and the population standard deviation is 2. The sample standard deviation is about 2.14.

Worked Example: Data 4, 8, 6, 5, 3

The mean is 5.2. Squared deviations from the mean are 1.44, 7.84, 0.64, 0.04, 4.84, which add up to 14.8. For a sample, divide by n − 1 = 4 to get a variance of 3.7 and a standard deviation of 1.924. For a whole population, divide by n = 5, giving a variance of 2.96 and a standard deviation of 1.720.

Sample or Population?

Use the population formula only when your data includes every member of the group you care about. When data is a subset, use the sample formula, which divides by n − 1 to avoid underestimating the spread.

Interpreting the Result

  • A small standard deviation means values cluster near the mean.
  • In a roughly bell-shaped distribution, about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean and 95% within two.
  • Outliers pull the standard deviation up quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I use sample vs population?

Use population when your data includes every member, and sample when it is a subset.

Why divide by n − 1 for samples?

It corrects the bias that comes from estimating the mean from the same data.

What is variance?

The square of the standard deviation.

What does a high standard deviation mean?

The data points are spread far from the average.

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Standard deviation is the square root of variance, which puts the spread back into the original units of the data.

Why divide by n − 1?

Dividing by n − 1 (Bessel's correction) corrects the bias when estimating population spread from a sample.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. It is a square root, so it is zero or positive.
