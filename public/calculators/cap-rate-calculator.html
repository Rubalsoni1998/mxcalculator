About the Cap Rate Calculator

The capitalization rate, or cap rate, is the most widely used measure for comparing income properties. It tells you the yearly return a property would produce if you bought it with all cash, expressed as a percentage of its price. Investors use it to compare apartments, rental houses, retail and office buildings quickly, and to judge whether an asking price is fair.

This calculator works in two directions. Choose Cap rate to find the rate from a price and the property's income and expenses. Choose Property value to find what a property is worth at a cap rate you want to earn. It also reports net operating income (NOI), effective gross income, the operating expense ratio and the gross rent multiplier, and charts where each rental dollar goes.

Cap Rate Formula

Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Property Value × 100
NOI = Effective Gross Income − Operating Expenses
Effective Gross Income = Gross Rent × (1 − Vacancy Rate)
Property Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate

Example: A rental building has $60,000 of gross rent and 5% vacancy, so effective income is $57,000. Operating costs are $6,000 tax, $1,800 insurance, $3,000 repairs, $1,200 utilities and 8% management ($4,560), a total of $16,560.

NOI = $57,000 − $16,560 = $40,440. If the price is $500,000, the cap rate is $40,440 ÷ $500,000 = 8.09%. At a 6% target cap rate the same NOI supports a value of $674,000.

What Counts as an Operating Expense

  • Included: property taxes, insurance, repairs and maintenance, utilities paid by the owner, property management, HOA dues, landscaping, snow removal, advertising and legal or accounting costs tied to operating the property.
  • Not included: mortgage principal and interest, depreciation, income taxes, and big one-time capital improvements such as a new roof. Many investors set aside a reserve for those inside maintenance.

What Is a Good Cap Rate?

There is no single good number. The right cap rate depends on the market, property type, condition and risk. As a rough guide:

Cap rateTypical meaning
Under 4%Low risk, low return. Often prime locations with strong demand and appreciation.
4% – 6%Stable properties in established, in-demand markets.
6% – 8%Balanced return and risk. Common for many rental markets.
8% and upHigher return, higher risk. May signal older buildings, weaker areas or more vacancy.

A higher cap rate is not automatically better. It often reflects more risk. Compare a property against similar properties in the same area, not against the whole country.

Other Numbers Shown

  • Operating expense ratio: Operating expenses divided by effective gross income. Many rental properties fall between 30% and 50%.
  • Gross rent multiplier (GRM): Price divided by gross annual rent. It is a quick screening number, but it ignores expenses and vacancy, so cap rate is more reliable.
  • Net operating income: Income left after running the property but before debt service.

Limits of the Cap Rate

Cap rate is a snapshot of one year. It does not consider financing, future rent growth, appreciation, taxes on your own income or major repairs. For a full picture, also look at cash-on-cash return, IRR and the cost of capital improvements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a cap rate in real estate?

The capitalization rate is a property's net operating income divided by its price or value. It shows the unlevered yearly return, which is what you would earn with no mortgage.

Does cap rate include the mortgage?

No. Cap rate ignores financing on purpose, so you can compare properties fairly regardless of how each buyer pays for them. To include financing, use cash-on-cash return.

How do I calculate NOI?

Take your yearly rental income, subtract vacancy losses to get effective gross income, then subtract all operating expenses such as taxes, insurance, repairs, utilities and management. Do not subtract mortgage payments or depreciation.

Is a higher cap rate better?

Not always. A higher cap rate means a higher income return relative to price, but it usually comes with more risk such as weaker locations, higher vacancy or older buildings. A lower cap rate often means safer income and better appreciation prospects.

What vacancy rate should I use?

Many investors assume 5% to 8% for residential rentals. Use your local market's actual vacancy data and the property's history when you have them.

How is property value found from a cap rate?

Divide NOI by the cap rate. A property with $40,000 of NOI in a market where similar buildings sell at a 6.5% cap rate is worth about $615,000.
