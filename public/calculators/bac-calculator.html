About the BAC Calculator

Blood alcohol concentration (BAC) is the percentage of alcohol in your blood. It rises with every drink and falls slowly as the liver breaks alcohol down, at roughly 0.015 percent per hour.

This calculator estimates your BAC using the Widmark formula from your body weight, gender, number of drinks and time spent drinking. It is meant for awareness and education only.

How to Use the BAC Calculator

  1. Enter your weight and select your gender.
  2. Enter the number of standard drinks and the hours since you started drinking.
  3. Click Calculate to see your estimated BAC and time to sober.

Widmark Formula

BAC = (alcohol in ounces × 5.14) ÷ (weight in lb × r) − 0.015 × hours, where r is about 0.73 for men and 0.66 for women. A standard US drink contains about 0.6 fluid ounces of pure alcohol: one 12 oz beer, 5 oz wine or 1.5 oz of spirits.

Example: A 170 lb man who has four standard drinks in two hours has a BAC of about (2.4 × 5.14) ÷ (170 × 0.73) − 0.03 = 0.099 − 0.03 = about 0.07 percent.

Effects at Different BAC Levels

  • 0.02–0.05: relaxed, slightly impaired judgment.
  • 0.08: legal driving limit in most US states; coordination and reaction time are clearly impaired.
  • 0.15 and above: serious impairment, vomiting and blackouts are common.
  • 0.30 and above: risk of unconsciousness, dangerously slowed breathing and death.

Worked Example: Four Drinks in Two Hours

A 180 lb man who has four standard drinks (about 14 grams of alcohol each) over two hours has an estimated BAC of about 0.071% using the Widmark formula with a 0.68 factor and 0.015% elimination per hour. That is already above the 0.08% limit that applies for driving in most US states.

Why Estimates Can Be Wrong

  • Food, medications, health conditions, body fat and drinking pace all change real BAC.
  • Women typically reach a higher BAC than men of the same weight for the same drinks, which the calculator reflects with a different factor.
  • Drink size varies. A large pour or a strong craft beer can count as more than one standard drink.

Safety

Impairment begins well before 0.08%. The only safe approach to driving is not to drink beforehand; plan a ride instead. Coffee, showers and food do not lower BAC faster. If someone cannot be woken or is breathing slowly, call 911.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the legal BAC limit for driving?

It is 0.08 percent in most of the US and in many other countries, lower in some places (0.05 in Utah and many countries, 0.04 for commercial drivers).

How long does it take to sober up?

Roughly 0.015 percent per hour on average. Coffee, showers or cold air do not speed this up.

Why do men and women differ?

Women generally have less body water and different enzyme activity, so the same drinks tend to produce a higher BAC.

Is this accurate enough to decide if I can drive?

No. Real BAC varies with food, medications, health and genetics. If you have been drinking, do not drive.

How long does it take to sober up?

The body clears roughly 0.015% BAC per hour on average, but this varies. Only time lowers BAC.

What counts as one standard drink?

In the US it is about 14 grams of pure alcohol: 12 oz of regular beer, 5 oz of wine or 1.5 oz of 80-proof spirits.

Can I use this to decide if I can drive?

No. It is an estimate and impairment can occur at lower levels. Do not drive after drinking.
