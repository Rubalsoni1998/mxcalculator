About the Mortgage Calculator

A mortgage is a loan used to buy a home, repaid in monthly installments over 15 to 30 years. Your payment usually covers principal and interest, plus property taxes, homeowners insurance and sometimes private mortgage insurance (PMI) or HOA fees.

This mortgage calculator turns your home price, down payment, loan term and interest rate into a monthly payment, a full amortization schedule and a total cost. You can also add extra monthly or yearly payments to see how much interest and time you save.

How to Use the Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the home price and your down payment (as an amount or percentage).
  2. Choose the loan term and enter the interest rate.
  3. Turn on taxes and costs to add property tax, insurance, PMI, HOA and other monthly costs.
  4. Optionally add extra monthly or yearly payments, then view the payment, schedule and totals.

Mortgage Payment Formula

M = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. Taxes, insurance and PMI are then added on top.

Example: On a $300,000 home with 20 percent down ($240,000 loan), a 30-year term and a 6 percent rate, the principal and interest payment is about $1,439 per month. Over 30 years you would pay roughly $278,000 in interest.

What Is Included in Your Monthly Payment

  • Principal: the part that reduces what you owe.
  • Interest: the lender's charge, largest in the early years.
  • Property tax and insurance: often collected in escrow.
  • PMI: usually required if your down payment is under 20 percent.
  • HOA fees: for condos and planned communities.

Ways to Lower Your Mortgage Cost

  • Increase your down payment to reduce the loan and avoid PMI.
  • Compare rates from several lenders and improve your credit score.
  • Choose a shorter term such as 15 years for far less total interest.
  • Make extra principal payments; even a small amount each month shortens the loan.

Worked Example: $400,000 Home, 20% Down

Say you buy a $400,000 home with 20% down. The loan is $320,000. At 6.5% for 30 years, principal and interest come to about $2,022.62 a month, and you would pay roughly $408,142 in interest over the life of the loan. Property tax, homeowners insurance and any HOA dues come on top of that, which is why the calculator lets you add them.

15 years instead of 30. Using an illustrative 5.75% rate (shorter terms usually price lower), the payment rises to about $2,657.31, but total interest falls to about $158,316. You pay roughly $635 more per month and save about $249,826 in interest.

Adding $200 a month. On the 30-year loan above, an extra $200 toward principal each month pays the loan off in about 23 years and 5 months and cuts interest by roughly $105,429.

The rates here are examples, not quotes. Use the rate on your own Loan Estimate.

Using the Calculator for Real Decisions

  • Compare loan offers on the same inputs. Change only the rate or term, so you see the true cost of each option.
  • Test your budget with a rate cushion. Rerun the numbers 0.5 to 1 percentage point higher than today's rate to see whether the payment still fits.
  • Include escrow items. Property tax and insurance vary a lot by state and county; see the property tax calculator for typical effective rates.
  • Check PMI. With less than 20% down, use the PMI calculator to see the added monthly cost and when it can end.

Costs Beyond the Monthly Payment

The payment is only part of owning a home. Plan for closing costs (see the closing cost calculator), maintenance, utilities and a cash reserve. Many buyers also underestimate how much the escrow portion can change from year to year when taxes or insurance are reassessed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much house can I afford?

A common guideline is to keep total housing costs under about 28 percent of gross monthly income and total debts under 36 percent. Lenders also look at credit and down payment.

Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better?

A 15-year loan has a higher payment but much less interest. A 30-year loan has a lower payment and more flexibility.

What is PMI and how do I avoid it?

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender when you put down less than 20 percent. You can avoid it with a larger down payment or remove it once you reach about 20 percent equity.

What is an amortization schedule?

It is a table showing how each payment splits between principal and interest over the life of the loan.

Do extra payments really help?

Yes. Extra payments go straight to principal, cutting total interest and shortening the loan term.

How much is the monthly payment on a $300,000 mortgage?

At 6.5% for 30 years, principal and interest on $300,000 is about $1,896.20 a month, before taxes, insurance and PMI. At other rates or terms, enter your numbers in the calculator above.

Does the calculator include property tax and insurance?

Yes. Enter annual property tax, home insurance, PMI and HOA fees to see an estimated total monthly payment, not just principal and interest.

Where can I find the real rate and costs for my loan?

Lenders must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days of your application. It lists the rate, monthly payment and closing costs so you can compare offers side by side.
