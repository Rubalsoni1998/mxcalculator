About the Inflation Calculator

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, which reduces what each dollar can buy. This calculator estimates the future cost of something worth a given amount today, and the buying power of that money later.

Enter the amount, an average inflation rate and the number of years.

How to Use the Inflation Calculator

  1. Enter the current amount.
  2. Enter the average yearly inflation rate and number of years.
  3. Click Calculate to see the future cost and lost buying power.

Inflation Formula

Future cost = amount × (1 + inflation rate)^years. Buying power = amount ÷ (1 + inflation rate)^years.

Example: At 3 percent inflation, something that costs $100 today costs $134.39 in 10 years, and $100 then buys only about $74.41 worth of today's goods.

Worked Example: 3% Inflation for 20 Years

At a steady 3% a year, prices rise by a factor of 1.0320 ≈ 1.81. Something that costs $1,000 today would cost about $1,806 in 20 years. Put the other way, $1,000 of cash would then buy only about $554 of today's goods.

The rule of 72 gives a quick check: divide 72 by the inflation rate to estimate the years it takes prices to double. At 3% that is about 24 years; at 4% it is about 18.

Why Your Own Inflation Rate Differs

The Consumer Price Index measures an average basket of goods. Your household may spend more on housing, health care, tuition or food than that average, so the inflation you feel can be higher or lower. Treat the calculator as a way to test scenarios, not to predict the future.

Using Inflation in Planning

  • Judge a raise in real terms: a 3% raise during 3% inflation does not increase buying power.
  • Think about "real" returns: an investment earning 6% while inflation runs 3% grows buying power by about 3% a year. See the investment calculator.
  • Plan retirement spending in future dollars with the retirement calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a normal inflation rate?

Many central banks target about 2 percent a year.

How does inflation affect savings?

If your savings earn less than inflation, their real value falls.

How can I protect against inflation?

Investments such as stocks, real estate, TIPS and inflation-linked bonds have historically helped.

Which inflation rate should I use?

Use a long-term average, for example 2 to 3 percent, or a specific measure such as CPI.

Where do official US inflation numbers come from?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Consumer Price Index each month. Annual inflation is the percentage change in the CPI over 12 months.

What is the formula for inflation?

Inflation rate = (new price level − old price level) ÷ old price level × 100. To project a future price, multiply today's price by (1 + rate) raised to the number of years.

Does inflation affect savings?

Yes. If your savings earn less than the inflation rate, their buying power shrinks even though the dollar balance grows.
