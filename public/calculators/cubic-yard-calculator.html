About the Cubic Yard Calculator

Bulk landscaping and construction materials such as mulch, topsoil, gravel, sand and concrete are sold by the cubic yard. This calculator converts the length, width and depth of your project into cubic yards, adds a settling allowance, and estimates weight in tons, bags and cost.

How the Calculation Works

Cubic feet = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (ft)
Circle: Cubic feet = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)² × Depth (ft)
Cubic yards = Cubic feet ÷ 27
Cubic meters = Cubic feet × 0.0283168
Tons = Cubic yards × Pounds per cubic yard ÷ 2,000

Example: A 10 by 10 foot area covered 6 inches deep with gravel is 10 × 10 × 0.5 = 50 cubic feet. Dividing by 27 gives 1.85 cubic yards. At about 1.4 tons per cubic yard, that is about 2.6 tons of gravel.

Cubic Yard Facts

One cubic yard is a cube 3 feet on each side, which equals exactly 27 cubic feet or about 0.7646 cubic meters. A cubic yard is a measure of volume, not weight, so the number of tons depends on the material. Dry gravel and sand weigh around 1.3 to 1.4 tons per cubic yard, topsoil about 1.0 to 1.35 tons, and wood mulch only 0.2 to 0.4 tons. Moisture and compaction change the real weight.

Key Terms

  • Cubic yard: A volume of 27 cubic feet. The standard unit for ordering bulk materials in the US.
  • Bulk density: The weight of a material per unit of volume. It lets you convert cubic yards to tons.
  • Settling allowance: Extra material to cover compaction and spillage. Loose material compacts after it is spread and watered.
  • Bag count: Retail bags are smaller. A typical mulch bag holds 2 cubic feet (about 13.5 bags per cubic yard), a topsoil bag 1 cubic foot (27 bags) and a gravel or sand bag 0.5 cubic feet (54 bags).

Tips for Ordering

  • Use the right depth. Topsoil for lawns is often 2 to 6 inches, mulch about 2 to 3 inches, gravel paths 2 to 3 inches and sand bases 1 to 2 inches.
  • Add 5 to 10%. It is cheaper to order a little extra than to pay for a second delivery.
  • Compare bulk and bags. Bagged material costs more per yard, so bulk delivery is usually cheaper for more than a few cubic yards.
  • Ask for the unit. Suppliers often sell gravel and sand by the ton and mulch and soil by the yard. Use the weight estimate to compare.
  • Check truck limits. Heavy materials such as gravel and concrete are limited by weight, not volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate cubic yards?

Multiply length, width and depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. For depth in inches, divide by 12 first. A 10 by 10 foot area, 6 inches deep, is 50 cubic feet or 1.85 cubic yards.

How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?

There are exactly 27 cubic feet in one cubic yard, because a cubic yard is 3 feet by 3 feet by 3 feet.

How many tons are in a cubic yard?

It depends on the material. Gravel is about 1.4 tons per cubic yard, dry sand about 1.3, topsoil about 1.0 to 1.35 and wood mulch about 0.2 to 0.4.

How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

About 2,700 to 2,800 pounds, or roughly 1.4 tons, depending on stone size and moisture.

How many bags are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. That is about 13.5 bags of 2 cubic foot mulch, 27 bags of 1 cubic foot topsoil or 54 bags of 0.5 cubic foot gravel or sand.

How much extra material should I order?

Most people add 5 to 10% for settling, compaction and spillage. Add more for rough ground or deep fills.

How do I calculate cubic yards for a circle?

Square the radius (half the diameter), multiply by pi and then by depth to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. This calculator does it when you pick the circle shape.

How many cubic yards do I need for a garden bed?

Multiply the bed length and width by the depth. A 4 by 8 foot bed filled 6 inches deep needs 16 cubic feet, which is about 0.6 cubic yards.
