About the Concrete Calculator

Ordering the right amount of concrete avoids costly shortages and waste. The amount you need is the volume of the space you are filling, usually given in cubic yards or cubic metres.

This concrete calculator finds the volume for common shapes and converts it into ready-mix yards or pre-mixed bags, with an allowance for waste.

How to Use the Concrete Calculator

  1. Choose the shape: slab, footing, wall, column or similar.
  2. Enter the length, width and thickness or diameter.
  3. Add a waste allowance, then click Calculate to see yards and bags.

Concrete Volume Formula

Volume = length × width × thickness. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet, a 60 lb bag about 0.45 and a 40 lb bag about 0.3.

Example: A 10 ft by 10 ft patio slab 4 inches thick is 33.3 cubic feet, or 1.23 cubic yards. Adding 10 percent for waste gives about 1.36 yards, or roughly 56 bags of 80 lb mix.

Tips for Ordering Concrete

  • Add 5 to 10 percent extra for uneven ground and spillage.
  • Use ready-mix delivery for jobs over about 1 cubic yard.
  • Check local code for required thickness and reinforcement.

What the Calculator Does and Does Not Include

The tool has two modes: slab (length and width in feet, thickness in inches) and column (diameter in inches, height in feet, and a quantity). It reports cubic feet, cubic yards and a bag count rounded up for 80 lb and 60 lb bags. It does not apply a waste percentage, so add your own margin to the result. A footing or a wall is also a rectangular box, so enter it in slab mode using its length, its width and its thickness.

Worked Examples

Slab. A 12 ft by 16 ft pad, 4 inches thick, is 12 x 16 x (4/12) = 64 cubic feet, or 2.37 cubic yards. That is 107 bags of 80 lb mix. With a 10 percent margin it becomes 70.4 cubic feet, 2.61 yards, or 118 bags.

Footing. A 20 ft run, 2 ft wide and 12 inches deep, is 40 cubic feet, or 1.48 cubic yards. That is 67 bags of 80 lb mix.

Round columns. Six piers 12 inches across and 4 feet deep: the area of a 1-foot circle is about 0.785 square feet, times 4 feet times 6 gives 18.85 cubic feet, or 0.70 cubic yards. That is 32 bags, or 35 with a 10 percent margin.

Bags or Ready-Mix?

One cubic yard of ready-mix covers about 81 square feet at 4 inches thick (27 x 12 / 4). Bag yields are approximate, and the numbers in this tool assume 0.6 cubic feet per 80 lb bag and 0.45 for a 60 lb bag. Check the yield printed on the bag you actually buy. As a rule of thumb, a job needing more than a few dozen bags is often easier and more consistent with delivered concrete, since mixing a large pour by hand is hard to finish before the concrete sets. Ask suppliers about minimum loads, short-load fees and delivery charges, because those vary by region and company.

Metric dimensions need converting to feet and inches first. The conversion calculator handles that, and the percent calculator is useful for working out your waste margin.

Getting Accurate Dimensions

  • Measure the finished thickness. Slab thickness is the poured depth, not the depth of the dug area. Gravel base and forms change the figure.
  • Allow for uneven ground. Sub-grades are rarely flat, and a slab that runs a quarter inch thick across the whole area adds more than you expect.
  • Break complex shapes into rectangles. Calculate each section, then add the totals.
  • Order a little extra. Running short mid-pour can leave a cold joint, which is a bigger problem than a surplus.

Required thickness, reinforcement, footing depth below the frost line and permits depend on your local building code and soil. For anything structural, confirm with your local building department or a licensed contractor or engineer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of concrete are in a yard?

About 45 bags of 80 lb mix, or 60 bags of 60 lb mix.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

Four inches is typical for patios and walkways, more for driveways and heavy loads.

How long does concrete take to cure?

It sets in about a day and reaches most of its strength in 28 days.

Do I need rebar?

Often yes for structural slabs. Ask your local building authority.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete make a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. At about 0.6 cubic feet per 80 lb bag, that is 45 bags. Bag yields vary by brand, so check the label on the bag you buy.

How do I calculate concrete for a slab in square feet?

Multiply the slab area in square feet by the thickness in feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 200 square foot slab at 4 inches is 200 x 0.333 = 66.7 cubic feet, or about 2.47 cubic yards.

How much extra concrete should I order?

Many builders add roughly 5 to 10 percent to cover uneven ground, spillage and slight form movement. Go toward the high end for rough sub-grades or complex shapes, and confirm with your supplier.

How do I calculate concrete for a round column or post hole?

Find the radius in feet (half the diameter, divided by 12 if in inches), square it, multiply by pi and by the depth in feet. Multiply by the number of holes, then divide by 27 for cubic yards.
