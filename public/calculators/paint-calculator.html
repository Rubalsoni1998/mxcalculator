About the Paint Calculator

Buying too little paint means a second trip to the store and a risk of slightly different batches. Buying too much wastes money. This calculator finds the wall area from your room size, subtracts doors and windows, and divides by the coverage printed on the can to tell you how many gallons to buy.

It can also add the ceiling, a coat of primer and extra for touch-ups, and it estimates the cost.

How the Calculation Works

Wall area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Ceiling height
Paintable area = Wall area − (Doors × 20) − (Windows × 15)
Gallons = Paintable area × Coats ÷ Coverage per gallon
Ceiling area = Length × Width
Primer gallons = Paintable area ÷ 250

Example: A 12 by 15 foot room with 8 foot ceilings has 432 square feet of wall. Two doors (40 sq ft) and two windows (30 sq ft) take away 70, leaving 362 sq ft to paint.

At 400 sq ft per gallon, one coat needs 0.91 gallons and two coats need 1.81 gallons, so buy 2 gallons. The 180 sq ft ceiling needs another 0.90 gallons for two coats.

How Much Does a Gallon of Paint Cover?

Most manufacturers rate a gallon at about 350 to 400 square feet on a smooth, primed surface. Sherwin-Williams and PPG estimate about 400, while Behr and Benjamin Moore calculators use about 350. Rough, porous or unprimed walls soak up more paint, and 300 to 350 square feet per gallon is a safer assumption. Textured ceilings can need 20 to 30% more. Exterior surfaces such as stucco and brick can drop to 150 to 250 square feet per gallon.

Key Terms

  • Coverage: The area one gallon covers in one coat. The label on the can gives the exact number.
  • Coat: One layer of paint. Two coats give the most even color and finish.
  • Primer: A base coat that seals the surface and helps paint stick and cover. It is worth using on bare drywall, stains and big color changes.
  • Cutting in: Brushing the edges before rolling the open wall. It uses little paint but takes time.

Tips to Buy the Right Amount

  • Measure each wall once. If the room is not a simple rectangle, add up each wall's width times height.
  • Round up to full gallons. Paint from the same batch matches better than a later purchase.
  • Check the can. The coverage printed on the label beats any rule of thumb.
  • Trim and doors need little. A quart usually covers trim, doors and windowsills for a room.
  • Keep a little extra. Store a small amount labeled with the room and color for touch-ups.
  • Use a tinted primer for dark colors. It can save a coat.

Estimates assume smooth interior walls. Results will vary with surface texture, color change and application method.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate how much paint I need?

Multiply the room perimeter by the ceiling height to get wall area, subtract about 20 square feet per door and 15 per window, then divide by the paint coverage (usually 350 to 400 square feet per gallon). Multiply by the number of coats.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350 to 400 square feet on smooth, primed walls. Rough or unprimed surfaces can need 300 square feet per gallon or less.

How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 by 12 room with 8 foot ceilings has about 384 square feet of wall. That is roughly one gallon for one coat or two gallons for two coats, before subtracting doors and windows.

How many coats of paint do I need?

Most interior jobs need two coats for even color. A single coat can work when repainting a similar color with a quality paint, and bright or dark colors may need more.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

You can. A typical door is about 20 square feet and a window about 15. Skipping the subtraction adds a small cushion, which many painters like for touch-ups.

How much paint do I need for the ceiling?

Multiply length by width to get ceiling area. A 12 by 15 foot ceiling is 180 square feet, which is about half a gallon per coat. Textured ceilings may need 20 to 30% more.

How much primer do I need?

Primer covers about 200 to 300 square feet per gallon, which is less than paint, so buy a little more primer than the paint math suggests. This calculator uses 250 square feet per gallon.

How much does a quart of paint cover?

About 88 to 100 square feet, enough for a small accent wall, trim or furniture project.
