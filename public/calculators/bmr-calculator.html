About the BMR Calculator

Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns each day just to stay alive: breathing, circulating blood, repairing cells and keeping organs working. It usually accounts for 60 to 70 percent of the calories you use in a day.

This BMR calculator estimates your resting energy use from your age, sex, height and weight, and can also use body fat percentage for a lean-mass based estimate. Knowing your BMR is the first step to setting a realistic calorie target for weight loss, maintenance or muscle gain.

How to Use the BMR Calculator

  1. Select your unit system and enter your age and gender.
  2. Enter your height and weight.
  3. Optionally enter your body fat percentage if you want the Katch-McArdle result.
  4. Click Calculate to see your BMR in calories per day.

BMR Equations

Mifflin-St Jeor: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5 for men and − 161 instead of + 5 for women.
Katch-McArdle: BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg).
The Harris-Benedict equation is an older formula with similar inputs.

Example: A 30-year-old woman who is 165 cm tall and weighs 65 kg has a Mifflin-St Jeor BMR of 650 + 1,031 − 150 − 161 = about 1,370 calories per day.

BMR vs TDEE

BMR covers only resting needs. Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) adds the calories used for movement, exercise and digesting food, so it is higher. Multiply BMR by an activity factor, or use the TDEE calculator, to find the calories needed to maintain your weight.

What Affects Your BMR

  • Muscle mass: muscle burns more energy at rest than fat.
  • Age: BMR tends to fall gradually with age.
  • Body size and sex: larger bodies and men usually have a higher BMR.
  • Genetics, hormones and temperature also play a part.

Two More Worked Examples

Man, 40 years old, 178 cm, 80 kg. Mifflin-St Jeor: 10 × 80 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 40 + 5 = 800 + 1,112.5 − 200 + 5 = 1,717.5 calories per day.

Woman, 52 years old, 160 cm, 58 kg. 10 × 58 + 6.25 × 160 − 5 × 52 − 161 = 580 + 1,000 − 260 − 161 = 1,159 calories per day.

Both results describe resting needs only. Your real daily burn will be several hundred calories higher once you add movement and food digestion.

Which Equation to Trust

Mifflin-St Jeor is widely used in clinical nutrition because it has held up well for people across a range of body sizes. Katch-McArdle can be more accurate for very lean or very muscular people, but only if your body fat percentage is reliable. A poor body fat estimate produces a poor Katch-McArdle result, so if you only have a rough guess, the Mifflin-St Jeor number is usually the safer choice. The two methods can differ by 100 to 200 calories, which is normal.

Common Mistakes

  • Eating exactly your BMR to lose weight. BMR is a floor for the body's basic work, not a diet target. Very low intakes can cost muscle and nutrients.
  • Treating it as exact. Measured resting metabolic rate varies from person to person even after accounting for age, sex, height and weight. Use the figure as a starting estimate and adjust based on real results over several weeks.
  • Mixing units. Mifflin-St Jeor expects kilograms and centimeters. Converting pounds and inches incorrectly is a common cause of results that look far off.
  • Using an old weight. After a significant weight change, recalculate. BMR drops as body mass drops.

BMR, RMR and Why Labs Differ

Strictly, BMR is measured after sleep, fasting and complete rest in a thermoneutral room. Resting metabolic rate (RMR) is measured under looser conditions and is typically a little higher. Most equations, including those here, are really predicting RMR. If you want a measured value, some clinics and university labs offer indirect calorimetry, which measures oxygen use and carbon dioxide output.

Turning BMR Into a Plan

Take your BMR to the TDEE calculator to include activity, or use the calorie calculator to see targets for loss or gain. Thyroid conditions, medicines and pregnancy can change energy needs, so talk with a clinician or registered dietitian if your results and real-world weight trends do not match.

How Age Changes the Result

In Mifflin-St Jeor, every extra year of age subtracts 5 calories from the daily estimate. Everything else equal, the 40-year-old man above would have a BMR about 50 calories higher at age 30, and about 50 calories lower at age 50. The decline is partly a loss of muscle, which is why staying active and doing strength work in midlife and later helps protect resting energy needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a normal BMR?

It varies widely. Many adult women have a BMR near 1,200 to 1,600 calories and many adult men near 1,500 to 2,000, depending on size, age and body composition.

Which BMR formula is most accurate?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is widely considered the most reliable for the general population. Katch-McArdle can be better if you know your body fat percentage.

Should I eat below my BMR to lose weight?

Eating well below your BMR for long periods is generally not recommended, as it can cause fatigue and muscle loss. Aim for a moderate deficit from your TDEE instead.

Can I increase my BMR?

Building muscle through strength training, eating enough protein and staying active can help support your metabolic rate.

Is BMR the same as RMR?

They are very close. RMR, resting metabolic rate, is measured under less strict conditions and is usually slightly higher than BMR.

Is a higher BMR better?

A higher BMR means your body uses more energy at rest, which gives you more room in your calorie budget. It is not a health score by itself. It mostly reflects body size, muscle mass, age and sex.

Can I increase my BMR?

Building muscle through resistance training raises resting energy use modestly, since muscle tissue costs more energy than fat. Large boosts are unrealistic, and extreme dieting can lower BMR. Supplements marketed to raise metabolism have little reliable support.

Why is my BMR lower than my friend's?

BMR depends on body size, muscle mass, age and sex. A smaller, older or less muscular person will usually have a lower BMR than a larger, younger or more muscular one, even with similar habits. Genetics also play a part.

Should I eat below my BMR to lose weight?

Eating below BMR is generally not recommended without medical supervision. Aim for a moderate deficit below your total daily energy expenditure instead, and do not drop under commonly cited minimums of 1,200 calories for women or 1,500 for men unless a clinician advises it.
