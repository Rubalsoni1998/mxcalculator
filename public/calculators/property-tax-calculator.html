About the Property Tax Calculator

Property tax is a yearly tax that cities, counties and school districts charge on real estate. It pays for schools, roads, police, fire protection and other local services, and it is one of the biggest ongoing costs of owning a home. This calculator estimates your yearly and monthly property tax three ways: from your state's average effective rate, from your own local tax rate, or from a mill rate. It also compares your home across states and projects your bill over 10 years. Updated November 21, 2026.

How Property Tax Is Calculated

Assessed Value = Home Value × Assessment Ratio − Exemptions
Annual Tax = Assessed Value × Local Tax Rate
Annual Tax = Assessed Value ÷ 1,000 × Mill Rate

A mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value. A 12-mill rate on a $350,000 assessed home gives 350 × $12 = $4,200 a year, or $350 a month.

Example: A $350,000 home in Texas, which has an average effective rate of 1.40%.

$350,000 × 1.40% = $4,900 a year, about $408 a month. The same home in New Jersey (1.88%) would owe about $6,580, and in Hawaii (0.29%) about $1,015.

Why Your Bill Can Differ From the State Average

  • Local rates: Counties, cities and school districts each add their own rate, so neighbors a few miles apart can pay very different amounts.
  • Assessed value: Your county assigns a value that may be below market value, and some states cap how fast it can rise.
  • Exemptions: Homestead, senior, veteran and disability exemptions reduce the taxable value.
  • Reassessment: A sale, a major renovation or a county-wide revaluation can reset your assessed value.

Ways to Lower Your Property Tax

  • Apply for every exemption you qualify for, such as homestead, senior and veteran relief. Deadlines are strict.
  • Appeal your assessment if comparable homes sold for less than your assessed value. Bring recent sales and, if needed, a private appraisal.
  • Check your bill for errors such as the wrong square footage, bedroom count or lot size.
  • Plan for escrow: If your lender pays your tax from escrow, review the yearly statement, since rising tax raises your monthly payment.

Sources: Tax Foundation state and county property tax data (2024), as reported by Rocket Mortgage (October 2026). Find your exact rate on your county assessor or tax collector website.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is property tax calculated?

Your county multiplies your home's assessed value by the local tax rate. Assessed value is the market value times the assessment ratio, minus any exemptions. With a mill rate, you multiply the assessed value by the mill rate and divide by 1,000.

What is a mill rate?

A mill is one dollar of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value. A rate of 15 mills means $15 per $1,000, or 1.5% of assessed value.

Which states have the highest property taxes?

New Jersey and Illinois lead with effective rates of 1.88%, followed by Connecticut at 1.54%, Vermont at 1.51% and New Hampshire at 1.50%, based on 2024 Tax Foundation data.

Which states have the lowest property taxes?

Hawaii has the lowest effective rate at 0.29%, then Alabama at 0.37%, with Utah and Arizona at 0.48% and South Carolina at 0.49%.

Can I deduct property tax on my federal return?

Property tax can be part of the state and local tax (SALT) deduction if you itemize. The SALT deduction has a dollar cap and phases down at higher incomes, so check current IRS guidance or ask a tax professional.

How do I appeal my property tax assessment?

Contact your county assessor for the appeal deadline and forms, then show that your assessed value is higher than the market value, using recent sales of similar homes nearby or a private appraisal.
