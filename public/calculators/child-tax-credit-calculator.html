About the Child Tax Credit Calculator

The Child Tax Credit (CTC) reduces your federal income tax for each qualifying child. For tax year 2026 the credit is worth up to $2,200 per child, and up to $1,700 of it can be refunded to you even if you owe no income tax. This calculator applies the income phase-out, uses the credit against your tax, then works out the refundable Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC). Updated November 21, 2026.

How the Credit Is Calculated

Credit = ($2,200 × qualifying children) + ($500 × other dependents)
Phase-out = $50 for each $1,000 (or part) of income over the threshold
Refundable ACTC = smaller of: unused credit, $1,700 × children, 15% × (earned income − $2,500)

Example: A married couple with two children, $90,000 of income and $5,000 of tax before credits.

The credit is 2 × $2,200 = $4,400. No phase-out applies below $400,000. All $4,400 is used to reduce the $5,000 tax, so they owe $600 after the credit and no refundable amount is needed.

Example (low tax): A single parent with one child, $30,000 of earnings and $500 of tax before credits.

The $2,200 credit wipes out the $500 tax. The $1,700 left over is limited to the smaller of $1,700 and 15% × ($30,000 − $2,500) = $4,125, so $1,700 comes back as a refund. Total benefit: $2,200.

The $2,200 maximum and $1,700 refundable amount were set by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and confirmed for 2026 in IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. The phase-out thresholds are fixed in law and are not adjusted for inflation.

Who Qualifies

  • The child is under age 17 at the end of the tax year.
  • The child is your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, sibling, or a descendant of one of them, and lives with you for more than half the year.
  • The child did not provide more than half of their own support and is claimed as your dependent.
  • The child has a Social Security number valid for work, issued before your return due date. You (and at least one spouse on a joint return) also need a Social Security number.
  • Your modified AGI is below the point where the credit phases out completely.

Children who do not qualify, such as a 17-year-old, may still qualify you for the $500 credit for other dependents.

CTC vs. Other Family Credits

  • Earned Income Tax Credit: A separate refundable credit for lower-income workers. You can claim both.
  • Child and Dependent Care Credit: Covers part of daycare or care costs so you can work.
  • Dependent care FSA: A pre-tax account for care expenses, which works alongside the credit.

Source: IRS Child Tax Credit and Rev. Proc. 2025-32.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Child Tax Credit for 2026?

Up to $2,200 per qualifying child under 17. Up to $1,700 of that amount can be refunded as the Additional Child Tax Credit if your credit is more than the income tax you owe.

Is the Child Tax Credit refundable?

Partly. The credit first reduces your income tax. If some credit is left, you can get up to $1,700 per child back, limited to 15% of your earned income above $2,500.

When does the Child Tax Credit phase out?

It starts to shrink when modified AGI passes $200,000 ($400,000 for married couples filing jointly). It falls by $50 for every $1,000 over the limit.

Does my child need a Social Security number?

Yes. For tax years 2025 and later, each qualifying child must have a Social Security number, and so must you (or at least one spouse on a joint return). Without one you may still claim the $500 credit for other dependents for the child.

What age must my child be?

The child must be under 17 at the end of the tax year. A child who turns 17 during the year does not qualify for the CTC, but may qualify you for the $500 other dependent credit.

What is the difference between the CTC and the ACTC?

The Child Tax Credit lowers the tax you owe. The Additional Child Tax Credit is the refundable part: money you receive when your credit is bigger than your tax bill.
