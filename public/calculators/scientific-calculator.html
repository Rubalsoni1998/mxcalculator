About the Scientific Calculator

A scientific calculator goes beyond basic arithmetic. It handles trigonometric functions, logarithms, exponents, roots, factorials and constants such as π and e.

This online version works on desktop and mobile with no download.

How to Use the Scientific Calculator

  1. Choose degree or radian mode.
  2. Type or tap numbers and functions.
  3. Press = to see the result.

Functions Available

Trigonometry (sin, cos, tan and inverses), logarithms (log, ln), powers (x², xʸ), roots (√), factorials (n!) and constants π and e.

Example: sin(30°) = 0.5, 2^10 = 1,024 and 5! = 120.

Common Gotchas

  • Degrees vs. radians. sin(30°) = 0.5, but sin(30 radians) = -0.9880. Check the mode before using trig functions.
  • Order of operations. 2 + 3 × 4 = 14, not 20. Use parentheses to group, for example (2 + 3) × 4 = 20.
  • log vs. ln. log is base 10 and ln is base e. log(1000) = 3, ln(e) = 1.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between degrees and radians?

They are two units for angles. 180 degrees equals π radians.

What is log vs ln?

log is base 10; ln is the natural logarithm, base e.

How do I calculate a factorial?

n! is the product of all positive integers up to n.

Does it follow order of operations?

Yes, brackets, exponents, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.

What does the factorial button do?

n! multiplies all whole numbers from 1 to n. For example, 5! = 120.

How do I calculate a square root?

Use the √ key, or raise the number to the power 0.5.

Why does my trig result look wrong?

The calculator may be in radians when you expect degrees. Switch the mode and try again.
