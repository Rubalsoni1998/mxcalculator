About the Weighted GPA Calculator

A weighted GPA gives extra credit for harder classes. The same A earns 4.0 in a regular course, 4.5 in an Honors course and 5.0 in an AP, IB or dual-enrollment course on the most common scale. This calculator applies your school's bonus to each class, then shows your weighted GPA next to your unweighted GPA, which many colleges also calculate.

How the Calculation Works

Grade points = Letter grade value + Level bonus (no bonus on an F)
Quality points = Grade points × Credits
Weighted GPA = Total quality points ÷ Total credits
Unweighted GPA = Σ(Letter grade value × Credits) ÷ Total credits

Example: Six one-credit classes: AP English B+ (3.3 + 1.0 = 4.3), AP Biology A− (3.7 + 1.0 = 4.7), Honors Algebra II B (3.0 + 0.5 = 3.5), and three regular classes with an A (4.0 each).

Total grade points are 4.3 + 4.7 + 3.5 + 4.0 + 4.0 + 4.0 = 24.5. Divided by 6 classes, the weighted GPA is 4.08. The unweighted GPA is 22.0 ÷ 6 = 3.67.

Common Weighting Scales

There is no national standard, and the College Board notes that high schools calculate GPA for advanced courses in different ways. The most common setup adds 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP, IB and dual-enrollment courses, which makes 5.0 the top of the scale. Some districts add only 0.5 for both, giving a 4.5 maximum, and a few add 1.0 for Honors too. Some schools do not weight at all.

Key Terms

  • Unweighted GPA: Every class counts on the standard 4.0 scale no matter how hard it is.
  • Weighted GPA: Adds bonus points for Honors, AP, IB or dual-enrollment classes, so it can pass 4.0.
  • Quality points: Grade points multiplied by the credits for a course.
  • Credits: How much a course counts. A year-long class is often 1.0 and a semester class 0.5.

Tips for Using Your GPA

  • Know which GPA a form wants. If it just says GPA, the 4.0 unweighted version is usually meant.
  • Do not skip rigor for a number. Colleges look at the courses you took as well as the GPA.
  • Use credits correctly. A half-credit class should count half as much as a full-credit class.
  • Test what-ifs. Change a grade or add next semester's classes to see how your GPA moves.
  • Ask your counselor. Each school's official GPA may follow its own rules, and the school's calculation is the one that appears on your transcript.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a weighted GPA?

Convert each letter grade to points, add the bonus for Honors, AP, IB or dual-enrollment classes, multiply by the credits, add everything up and divide by the total credits.

What is the weighted GPA scale?

Most schools use a 5.0 scale: an A is worth 4.0 in regular classes, 4.5 in Honors and 5.0 in AP or IB. Some schools use a 4.5 or 6.0 scale, so check your own school.

Is a 4.0 weighted GPA good?

On a 5.0 scale a 4.0 weighted GPA means roughly a B+ to A− average across challenging classes, which is solid. Colleges also look at your course rigor, test scores and activities.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

An unweighted GPA ignores course difficulty and stays on a 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for advanced classes and can go above 4.0.

Do colleges use weighted or unweighted GPA?

Many colleges recalculate GPA on their own scale or look at both. Some, such as the University of California, compute their own GPA from selected courses and do not use plus and minus grades.

Do Honors classes get 0.5 or 1.0 extra points?

The most common choice is 0.5 for Honors and 1.0 for AP, IB and dual enrollment, but a few districts give 1.0 to Honors too. Set the bonus in the calculator to match your school.

Can you get above a 4.0 GPA?

Yes, on a weighted scale. A student with all A grades in AP classes can reach 5.0 on the common scale, and some schools allow even higher.

How do credits affect GPA?

Credits set how much each class counts. A full-year class with 1.0 credit has twice the effect of a semester class with 0.5 credit.
