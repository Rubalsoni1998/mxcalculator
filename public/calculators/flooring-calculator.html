About the Flooring Calculator

Every flooring job produces waste from end cuts, odd corners and the occasional damaged piece. Order too little and you risk a mismatch on the second batch. This calculator measures your floor area, adds a waste allowance that fits your material and layout, and tells you how many boxes to buy and what the project will cost.

How the Calculation Works

Floor area = (Length × Width) + Additional area − Excluded area
Square feet to buy = Floor area × (1 + Waste %)
Boxes = Square feet to buy ÷ Coverage per box, rounded up
Material cost = Boxes × Coverage per box × Price per sq ft

Example: A 12 by 15 foot room is 180 sq ft. Adding 10% waste gives 198 sq ft. With boxes of 20 sq ft, you need 9.9 boxes, so buy 10 boxes, which covers 200 sq ft and leaves 20 sq ft after the room is covered.

How Much Extra Flooring to Buy

Common guidance is to buy 10% more than the measured area, and 5 to 10% for simple rooms with easy-to-cut materials. Diagonal, herringbone and patterned layouts need about 15% or more because nearly every edge piece is cut at an angle. Tile adds breakage, so 10% is the minimum and 15 to 20% is common for diagonal tile.

Key Terms

  • Waste factor: The extra percentage you add to cover cuts, mistakes and defects.
  • Box coverage: The square feet in one carton. You buy whole boxes, so you often end up with slightly more than you need.
  • Straight lay: Planks or tiles run parallel to a wall, which makes the least waste.
  • Diagonal or herringbone: Angled layouts that use more material.
  • Dye lot: A production batch. Pieces from different lots can differ in color, which is why leftover boxes are valuable for repairs.

Tips Before You Order

  • Measure twice. Break odd-shaped rooms into rectangles and add them with the additional area box.
  • Buy everything in one order. It keeps the color and dye lot consistent.
  • Keep spare boxes. Leftovers are useful for repairs later, since styles get discontinued.
  • Let flooring acclimate. Hardwood and laminate usually need to sit in the room for a few days before installation, as the maker directs.
  • Ask about returns. Some stores accept unopened boxes, which lets you order a bit more safely.
  • Do not forget extras. Underlayment, trim, transitions and adhesive are not in this estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate how much flooring I need?

Multiply room length by width in feet to get square feet, adjust for closets or fixed obstacles, add a waste percentage, and divide by the square feet in a box. Round up to whole boxes.

How much extra flooring should I buy?

Plan on about 10% extra for most materials. Simple rooms with laminate or vinyl may need only 5 to 10%, while diagonal or herringbone layouts and tile often need 15% or more.

How many boxes of flooring do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 by 12 room is 144 square feet. With 10% waste you need about 158 square feet, which is 8 boxes of 20 square feet or 7 boxes of 25 square feet.

Do I need more flooring for a diagonal layout?

Yes. Diagonal and herringbone patterns create angled cuts at every edge, so plan on at least 15% extra for hardwood and 15 to 20% for tile.

Should I include closets in my measurement?

Yes, if the closet will get the same flooring. Measure its length and width and enter the total in the additional area box.

Why is buying extra flooring a good idea?

It covers cutting waste and mistakes, and it gives you matching spare material for future repairs, since the exact color or style may be discontinued later.

Does this include underlayment or trim?

No. This calculator covers the flooring itself. Underlayment, baseboards, transitions and adhesive are separate purchases.

How do I figure out the cost?

Multiply the square feet you buy (boxes times coverage per box) by the price per square foot. Add installation if you hire it out. The calculator does both when you enter the prices.
