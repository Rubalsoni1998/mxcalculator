About the Target Heart Rate Calculator

Your target heart rate is the range at which your heart works effectively during exercise. Training in the right zone helps you build endurance, burn fat and avoid overdoing it.

This calculator estimates your maximum heart rate from your age and gives training zones for light, moderate and vigorous effort. Add your resting heart rate to use the Karvonen method.

How to Use the Target Heart Rate Calculator

  1. Enter your age.
  2. Optionally enter your resting heart rate for the Karvonen method.
  3. Click Calculate to see your zones.

Heart Rate Formulas

Maximum heart rate = 220 − age (a Tanaka estimate uses 208 − 0.7 × age). Karvonen: Target = (max HR − resting HR) × intensity + resting HR.

Example: For a 40-year-old, max heart rate is 180. Moderate exercise at 50 to 70 percent is 90 to 126 bpm and vigorous exercise at 70 to 85 percent is 126 to 153 bpm.

Heart Rate Zones

  • 50–60%: warm-up and recovery
  • 60–70%: fat burning and base endurance
  • 70–80%: aerobic fitness
  • 80–90%: hard intervals and performance

Worked Example: Age 40

Using the common formula 220 − age, a 40-year-old has an estimated maximum heart rate of 180 beats per minute. A moderate-intensity zone of 50 to 70 percent is about 90 to 126 bpm, and a vigorous zone of 70 to 85 percent is about 126 to 153 bpm.

Karvonen method. With a resting heart rate of 60, the heart rate reserve is 180−60 = 120. At 60% intensity the target is 60 + 0.60 × 120 = 132 bpm; at 80% it is 156 bpm.

Limits of the Formulas

Maximum heart rate varies from person to person by many beats per minute, so age-based formulas are rough. Medications such as beta-blockers lower heart rate and make these zones unreliable. If you have a heart condition, check zones with your doctor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good target heart rate for exercise?

Typically 50 to 85 percent of your maximum heart rate, depending on goals and fitness.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It is a rough estimate that can be off by 10 to 20 beats. A fitness test is more accurate.

What is the fat burning zone?

Around 60 to 70 percent of max heart rate, though total calories matter more than zone.

How do I find my resting heart rate?

Measure your pulse for 60 seconds first thing in the morning before getting up.

What is a good target heart rate for fat burning?

There is no special fat-burning zone. Moderate effort, roughly 50 to 70 percent of maximum, is sustainable for longer sessions. Total energy use over time matters most.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It is an estimate with a wide margin of error for individuals. A supervised test gives a better measurement.

What is a normal resting heart rate?

Adults commonly have a resting rate of 60 to 100 beats per minute, and well-trained people are often lower. Talk to a clinician about readings outside your usual range.
