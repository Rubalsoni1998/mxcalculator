About the EITC Calculator

The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) is a refundable federal credit for workers with low to moderate income. Because it is refundable, you get the full amount even if it is larger than the tax you owe. This calculator estimates your credit for tax year 2026 (the return you file in 2027) or tax year 2025, using the amounts the IRS published in Revenue Procedure 2025-32. It also shows how your credit changes as your income rises. Updated November 21, 2026.

How the EITC Is Calculated

The credit has three stages. It phases in as you earn more, stays at its maximum across a middle range, then phases out as income climbs.

Phase-in: Credit = Earned Income × Credit Rate (7.65% with no children, 34% with one, 40% with two, 45% with three or more)
Phase-out: Credit = Max Credit − Phase-out Rate × (Higher of AGI or Earned Income − Threshold)
Phase-out rates are 7.65% with no children, 15.98% with one child and 21.06% with two or more.

Who Qualifies

  • Earned income: You must have income from work, including wages, tips, overtime and self-employment. Starting with the 2025 rules, the IRS says to include the full amount of tip and overtime income when testing for the EITC, even if you can deduct part of it.
  • Income limits: Both AGI and earned income must be under the limit for your filing status and number of children.
  • Investment income: $12,200 or less for 2026.
  • Age with no children: At least 25 and under 65 at the end of the year.
  • Identification and residency: A valid Social Security number for you, your spouse and any qualifying child, and a US home for more than half the year.
  • Filing status: Married couples generally must file jointly. Some separated spouses who file separately can qualify if they meet IRS tests.

Tips to Claim the Credit Correctly

  • File even if you owe nothing. You have to file a return to receive the credit.
  • Use the right tax year. Use the limits for the year of the income, not the year you file.
  • Check your qualifying child. Only one person can claim a child for the EITC. Review the relationship, age and residency rules before you file.
  • Expect a short delay. By law the IRS cannot issue refunds that include the EITC or Additional Child Tax Credit before mid-February.
  • Check your state. Many states have their own EITC, often a percentage of the federal credit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum EITC for 2026?

For tax year 2026 the maximum credit is $664 with no qualifying children, $4,427 with one child, $7,316 with two children and $8,231 with three or more. You claim it on the return you file in 2027.

What is the EITC income limit for 2026?

The AGI limit for a single filer or head of household is $19,540 with no children, $51,593 with one child, $58,629 with two and $62,974 with three or more. For married couples filing jointly the limits are $26,820, $58,863, $65,899 and $70,244.

Is the Earned Income Tax Credit refundable?

Yes. If the credit is more than the tax you owe, the IRS pays you the difference as a refund.

What is the investment income limit for the EITC?

For 2026 your investment income must be $12,200 or less. It was $11,950 for 2025. Going over by even $1 disqualifies you for the year.

Can I get the EITC without children?

Yes, if you are at least 25 and under 65, have earned income, and meet the income limits. The credit is small, with a $664 maximum for 2026.

When will I get my EITC refund?

The IRS cannot release refunds that include the EITC or Additional Child Tax Credit until mid-February. Filing electronically with direct deposit is the fastest route once refunds begin.
