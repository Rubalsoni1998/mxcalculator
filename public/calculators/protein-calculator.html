About the Protein Calculator

Protein builds and repairs muscle, supports your immune system and keeps you full between meals. The official minimum is about 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight, but active people and those building muscle often benefit from more.

This protein calculator estimates your daily protein range from your weight, age, sex and activity level.

How to Use the Protein Calculator

  1. Enter your weight, age and gender.
  2. Select your activity level and goal.
  3. Click Calculate to see your daily protein target in grams.

Protein Formula

Protein (g) = body weight (kg) × grams per kg. Typical ranges are 0.8 g/kg for sedentary adults, 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg for active people and 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg for muscle gain.

Example: A 70 kg person lifting weights at 1.6 g/kg needs 70 × 1.6 = 112 grams of protein per day.

Good Protein Sources

  • Eggs, chicken, fish, lean beef and dairy
  • Beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu and tempeh
  • Nuts, seeds and whole grains such as quinoa

Spreading protein over three or four meals helps muscle repair.

Converting Pounds to Kilograms

Protein targets are given per kilogram, but US scales read in pounds. Divide pounds by 2.2046 to get kilograms, or multiply pounds by 0.4536. You can also use grams per pound directly: 0.8 g/kg is about 0.36 g per pound, and 1.6 g/kg is about 0.73 g per pound.

Worked Example: Same Person, Three Goals

A person weighing 175 lb is 175 × 0.4536 = 79.4 kg. At the 0.8 g/kg minimum, that is about 64 g per day. At 1.2 g/kg for regular exercise, about 95 g. At 1.6 g/kg while lifting weights, about 127 g. Using 2.2 g/kg during a calorie deficit gives about 175 g.

The jump from 64 to 127 grams is large, so it helps to look at what that means in food. A cooked chicken breast of about 6 ounces has roughly 40 to 50 grams, a cup of Greek yogurt roughly 17 to 20, a large egg about 6, and a cup of cooked lentils about 18. Labels differ, so check yours.

Which Weight Should You Use?

For people with a lot of body fat, using total body weight can overshoot. Some dietitians use a goal weight or an adjusted weight instead. If your result seems very high, a registered dietitian can help choose a sensible reference weight.

Common Mistakes

  1. Eating most protein at dinner. A more even spread across meals is easier to hit and may support muscle repair better.
  2. Assuming more is always better. Beyond the ranges here, extra protein adds calories without clear extra benefit for most people.
  3. Ignoring total calories. Protein targets sit inside a calorie budget. Pair this with the calorie calculator so protein does not crowd out other nutrients.

Who Should Ask Before Raising Protein

Adults with chronic kidney disease, a history of kidney stones, liver disease, or those on dialysis may need a limit set by their care team. If you are unsure, check your kidney function with your clinician. The GFR calculator explains one kidney measure, though it cannot replace medical evaluation. Pregnancy, breastfeeding and growing teens also have different needs, so follow your health provider's advice.

Protein Across the Day

A simple pattern is to aim for roughly equal servings at each meal. For a 127 gram target, that is about 30 to 40 grams at each of three or four eating occasions. Examples include eggs and Greek yogurt at breakfast, a chicken or tofu serving at lunch and fish or beans at dinner. Plant-based eaters can combine legumes, soy foods, nuts and grains across the day to cover their needs.

Using Your Result

Treat the output as a range rather than a pass or fail number. Landing within 10 to 15 percent of the target on most days is fine. If your appetite is low, adding one or two protein-rich snacks is often easier than eating larger meals. Revisit the number if your weight, training load or health status changes substantially.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein do I need per day?

At least 0.8 g per kg of body weight. Active adults and those losing weight often aim for 1.2 to 2.0 g per kg.

Can you eat too much protein?

Healthy adults handle high-protein diets well, but people with kidney disease should follow medical advice.

Do older adults need more protein?

Yes, about 1.0 to 1.2 g per kg is often advised to help maintain muscle.

Is plant protein as good as animal protein?

Plant protein works well when you eat a variety of sources, though you may need a bit more of it.

How much protein do I need per pound of body weight?

The minimum is about 0.36 grams per pound. Active people often aim for 0.5 to 0.9 grams per pound. For a 150 pound person, that is roughly 54 grams at the minimum and 75 to 135 grams for active goals.

How much protein can the body absorb in one meal?

The body absorbs essentially all the protein you eat, though how much is used for muscle building at one time is limited. Spreading protein over several meals is common advice, but there is no hard cap on what you can eat at once.

Do I need protein powder?

No. Food can meet protein needs for most people. Powders are a convenient option when you struggle to reach your target. Choose products with a short ingredient list and check that they fit within your calorie and sugar goals.

Is high protein safe for healthy kidneys?

Research in healthy adults has not shown that higher protein harms normal kidneys, but people with existing kidney disease should follow medical advice. If you have risk factors such as diabetes or high blood pressure, ask your clinician first.
