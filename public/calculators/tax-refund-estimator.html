About the Federal Tax Refund Estimator

Your refund is the difference between the tax you actually owe for the year and what you have already paid through paycheck withholding and estimated payments. This estimator works through your return the way the IRS does: income, adjustments, deductions, tax brackets, credits and payments. It applies the 2026 and 2025 rules, including the new deductions for tips, overtime, seniors and car loan interest that came from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Updated November 21, 2026.

How a Tax Refund Is Calculated

AGI = Wages + Other Income − Adjustments
Taxable Income = AGI − (Standard or Itemized Deduction) − Extra Deductions
Total Tax = Tax from Brackets − Non-Refundable Credits
Refund = Withholding + Estimated Payments + Refundable Credits − Total Tax

Example: A single filer with $60,000 of wages and $6,500 of federal tax withheld, for 2026.

The standard deduction is $16,100, so taxable income is $43,900. The tax is 10% of the first $12,400 plus 12% of the rest, or $5,020. The refund is $6,500 − $5,020 = $1,480.

New Deductions for 2025 to 2028

  • Senior deduction: $6,000 per person age 65 or older, reduced by 6% of income above $75,000 ($150,000 joint).
  • Tips: Up to $25,000 of qualified tips, phased out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint).
  • Overtime: Up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint) of qualified overtime premium pay, with the same phase-out.
  • Car loan interest: Up to $10,000 on a new US-assembled personal vehicle, phased out above $100,000 ($200,000 joint).
  • SALT cap: The state and local tax deduction cap is $40,400 for 2026 for itemizers, and phases down above $505,000 of income.

These deductions are claimed on Schedule 1-A and reduce taxable income, not AGI. This estimator uses simple versions of the rules and does not check every eligibility test.

Child Tax Credit

The Child Tax Credit is $2,200 per qualifying child under 17 for 2025 and 2026, and $500 for each other dependent. It begins to phase out at $200,000 of income ($400,000 joint). Up to $1,700 per child is refundable as the Additional Child Tax Credit, limited to 15% of earnings above $2,500.

Why Your Refund Might Be Smaller Than Expected

  • Lower withholding: If your employer withholds less than your tax, the refund shrinks. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator helps you adjust your W-4.
  • Side income: Freelance and gig income has no withholding and also owes self-employment tax, which this estimator does not calculate.
  • Lost credits: Children aging out, income above a phase-out or a missing Social Security number can reduce a credit.
  • Unemployment or retirement income: These may have little or no tax withheld.

Sources: IRS inflation adjustments for tax year 2026 (Revenue Procedure 2025-32) and IRS guidance on the One Big Beautiful Bill provisions and Schedule 1-A.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I estimate my federal tax refund?

Subtract your deductions from your income to find taxable income, apply the tax brackets, subtract your credits, and compare the result with the tax you already paid through withholding. If you paid more, the difference is your refund.

What is the standard deduction for 2026?

It is $16,100 for single filers and married people filing separately, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $24,150 for heads of household. It was $15,750, $31,500 and $23,625 for 2025.

Does this include the new tips, overtime and senior deductions?

Yes. Enter your qualified tips, overtime premium pay and car loan interest, and choose how many taxpayers are 65 or older. The estimator applies the caps and income phase-outs.

Why is my refund different from last year?

Changes in withholding, income, filing status, dependents or credits all change the result. New deductions and a higher standard deduction can also raise a refund even when your pay is the same.

Is the Child Tax Credit refundable?

Partly. For 2026 up to $1,700 per child can come back as a refund through the Additional Child Tax Credit, limited to 15% of your earned income above $2,500.

Does this estimator work for self-employed people?

Only in part. Enter your net profit under other income and your half of self-employment tax as an adjustment, but the estimator does not calculate self-employment tax. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator for a fuller picture.
