About the Car Sales Tax Calculator

When you buy a car you usually pay a state sales tax, a county or city tax in many places, and sometimes a separate excise, title or highway use tax. The rules change from state to state, including how a trade-in lowers your tax. This calculator uses the current rules for all 50 states, shows how much your trade-in saves, and compares your purchase with the highest and lowest tax states. Updated November 21, 2026.

How Car Sales Tax Is Calculated

Taxable Amount = Vehicle Price − Trade-In Credit (where allowed)
Sales Tax = Taxable Amount × (State Rate + Local Rate)

Example: A $35,000 car with an $8,000 trade-in bought from a dealer in Texas, which charges 6.25% statewide.

The taxable amount is $27,000, so the tax is $27,000 × 6.25% = $1,687.50. The trade-in saves $500 in tax. In California the same sale would be taxed on the full $35,000 because California gives no trade-in credit.

Does a Trade-In Lower Your Car Sales Tax?

  • No credit: California, Oklahoma and Virginia tax the full price even with a trade-in.
  • Dealer sales only: Georgia, Idaho, Illinois and Massachusetts give the credit only when you buy from a dealer.
  • Capped: Michigan limits the credit to $12,000 in 2026.
  • New vehicles only: Ohio counts a trade-in only toward a new vehicle.
  • Full credit: Most other states tax only the difference between the price and your trade-in.

If you live in a state with no trade-in credit, you may get more by selling your old car privately.

Special Rules This Calculator Applies

  • Connecticut: 7.75% instead of 6.35% on vehicles over $50,000, measured on the price before trade-in.
  • Florida: The county surtax of up to 2% applies only to the first $5,000 of the price.
  • Tennessee: Local tax applies only to the first $1,600, plus a 2.75% state tax on the next $1,600 (a $44 maximum).
  • South Carolina: The 5% fee is capped at $500.
  • Virginia: The 4.15% tax has a $75 minimum.
  • Washington: The 6.5% state rate plus a 0.5% motor vehicle sales tax that began on January 1, 2026.

Other rules apply to special cases: New Jersey adds 0.4% on new cars of $45,000 or more or under 19 mpg, Georgia taxes the fair market value rather than the price, and private sales in Texas are taxed on at least 80% of the state's standard presumptive value. Title, registration and plate fees are extra in every state.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is sales tax on a car?

It depends on your state and county. State rates run from 0% in Alaska, Montana and New Hampshire to 7.25% in California, and many places add a local tax. On a $30,000 car the state tax ranges from $0 to $2,175.

Do I pay car sales tax where I buy or where I live?

You pay where the car will be registered, which is usually your home address. Buying from a dealer in a lower-tax county or state does not lower your tax.

Does a trade-in reduce sales tax?

In most states yes, you are taxed only on the price minus your trade-in. California, Oklahoma and Virginia do not allow it, Michigan caps it at $12,000, Ohio allows it only on new vehicles, and some states require a dealer sale.

Which states have no sales tax on cars?

Alaska, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no general state sales tax on cars, though Alaska cities and boroughs charge their own and Oregon taxes new vehicles at 0.5%.

Do I pay sales tax on a car bought from a private seller?

Yes. You pay the tax to your state when you title and register the car. Some states, such as Illinois, use a flat vehicle use tax based on price and age instead.

Is the dealer documentation fee taxed?

It depends on the state. California and Florida tax it, and some states cap it. Ask for an itemized buyer's order so you can see the fee and the tax separately.
