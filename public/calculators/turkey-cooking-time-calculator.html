About the Turkey Cooking Time Calculator

Getting the timing right is the hardest part of cooking a turkey. Too short and the meat is unsafe, too long and it dries out, and a bird that is late holds up the whole dinner. This calculator turns your turkey's weight, whether it is thawed or frozen, whether it is stuffed, and your cooking method into a cooking time range. You also enter the time you want to eat, and it counts backward to tell you when to preheat the oven and when to put the bird in.

It also gives you thawing times if the turkey is still frozen, the number of servings to expect, and the safe temperature to look for. Thanksgiving in the United States is the fourth Thursday of November (November 26 in 2026), but the guidance works for any holiday or Sunday roast.

How Cooking Time Is Estimated

Oven times for a 325°F oven follow the timetable published by the USDA for whole turkeys, with times between the listed weights worked out in a smooth line. Other settings adjust that baseline:

  • 350°F oven: about 10% less time than at 325°F.
  • 375°F oven: about 18% less time. Cover the breast with foil if the skin browns too fast.
  • Frozen turkey: at least 50% longer than a thawed one, according to USDA guidance.
  • Deep frying: about 3 to 4 minutes per pound in 350°F oil, only for a fully thawed, unstuffed turkey of 14 lb or less.

Safe Internal Temperature

Cook the turkey until a food thermometer reads 165°F (74°C) in three places: the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh and the innermost part of the wing. If the bird is stuffed, the center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F. Do not rely on the pop-up timer or on the color of the juices. Start checking about 30 minutes before the shortest estimated time.

How to Thaw a Frozen Turkey Safely

  • In the refrigerator (best method): allow about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds, with the turkey in its wrapper on a tray at 40°F or below. A 16 lb bird needs about 3 to 4 days. Once thawed it can stay in the fridge for 1 to 2 days before cooking.
  • In cold water: allow about 30 minutes per pound. Keep the bird in a leak-proof bag, fully submerged, and change the water every 30 minutes. Cook it right after thawing.
  • In the microwave: follow your microwave's manual and cook immediately. This only suits small birds.
  • Never thaw on the counter. The outside warms into the danger zone while the center is still frozen.

How Big a Turkey Should You Buy?

Plan on 1 to 1.5 pounds of whole turkey per person. At the low end you get a normal dinner, at the high end you get generous portions and leftovers. A 16 lb turkey feeds about 10 to 16 people.

Tips for a Better Turkey

  • Pat the skin dry and brush with oil or butter for crisp, golden skin.
  • Use a roasting rack so hot air circulates under the bird.
  • Tent with foil if the breast browns before the thighs are done.
  • Rest the turkey 20 to 30 minutes, loosely covered. It stays hot, carves more cleanly and keeps its juices. The oven is also free for sides during this time.
  • Cook stuffing separately for easier, safer timing. If you do stuff the bird, fill it loosely just before roasting.
  • Plan for the longest time. A turkey that finishes early can rest under foil and towels for up to an hour. One that finishes late cannot be hurried.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to cook a 16 lb turkey?

An unstuffed 16 lb turkey roasted at 325°F takes about 3¾ to 4¼ hours. A stuffed one takes about 4 to 4¼ hours. Always check that the thickest part of the breast and thigh reach 165°F.

How long should I let a turkey rest?

Rest it 20 to 30 minutes, loosely covered with foil. It will stay hot, and the juices redistribute so the meat is moister when carved.

Can I cook a turkey from frozen?

Yes, it is safe, but it takes at least 50% longer than a thawed bird. Do not stuff a frozen turkey, and never deep-fry one. Remove the giblet bag once the bird has thawed enough to reach it.

What temperature should turkey be cooked to?

The USDA says 165°F (74°C) measured with a food thermometer in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh and the wing. Stuffing inside the bird must also reach 165°F.

Should I cook the turkey at 325°F or 350°F?

325°F is the standard in USDA guidance and is gentle and even. 350°F is a bit faster and is also safe. Whichever you pick, rely on the thermometer rather than the clock.

How many pounds of turkey per person?

Plan on 1 to 1.5 pounds per person. Choose the higher end if you want leftovers.

Is it safe to deep fry a turkey?

Deep frying is risky. Use a fully thawed, dry, unstuffed bird of 14 lb or less, fry outdoors on level ground away from structures, and never leave the pot unattended. Oil that spills or overflows can cause fires.
