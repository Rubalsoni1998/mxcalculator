About the Army Body Fat Calculator

The U.S. Army uses a tape measurement method to estimate body fat when a soldier exceeds the screening weight for their height. The result is compared with a maximum allowed percentage for the soldier's age and sex.

This calculator follows the same circumference approach. Enter your height, neck, waist and (for women) hip measurements and it estimates your body fat percentage and compares it with the standard.

How to Use the Army Body Fat Calculator

  1. Select your gender and enter your age.
  2. Enter your height, neck and waist measurements (and hips if female).
  3. Click Calculate to see your estimated body fat and whether it is within the standard.

How the Tape Test Works

The estimate uses the logarithm of circumference differences. For men the key value is waist − neck; for women it is waist + hips − neck. Height is also included. This is the same family of equation as the Navy body fat method.

Example: A man who is 70 inches tall with a 36-inch waist and a 15.5-inch neck has a circumference value of 20.5 inches, which gives an estimated body fat in the low 20 percent range.

Worked Example: Male, 70 in Tall

Take a man who is 70 inches tall with a 15.5 in neck and a 34 in waist (measured at the navel). The tape formula, 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76, gives about 16.5% body fat. If the waist were 32 in, the result would be about 12.2%, so a two-inch change in the waist moves the estimate by roughly 4.3 points.

Measuring Correctly

  • Measure the neck just below the larynx, with the tape sloping slightly downward.
  • Measure the waist at the navel for men; women's formulas also use hip circumference.
  • Keep the tape snug but not compressing the skin, and exhale normally.
  • Take each measurement three times and use the average.

What the Result Means

The tape test is a screening estimate and can differ from DEXA or other direct methods by several percentage points. Official standards depend on age and sex and are set by the branch of service, so check the current regulation rather than relying on a general calculator. Frequently Asked Questions

Who has to take the Army tape test?

Soldiers who exceed the screening table weight for their height may be measured with the tape test to check body composition.

How do I measure for the tape test?

Use a non-stretch tape. Measure the neck just below the larynx, the waist at the navel for men or at the narrowest point for women, and the hips at the widest point. Take three readings and average them.

Is this an official result?

No. This is an estimate for planning only. Only an official measurement by qualified personnel counts.

How can I reduce my tape test result?

A calorie deficit, strength training and cardio gradually reduce waist size, which lowers the estimate.

Is the Army tape test accurate?

It is a quick estimate. Accuracy depends on measuring technique and can differ from direct methods by several percentage points.

What measurements do women need for the tape test?

Women are measured at the neck, waist and hips, in addition to height. Always confirm the current method in the official regulation.

Can muscle affect the tape result?

The formula only uses circumferences, so a thick neck and a thin waist lower the estimate, while a larger waist raises it regardless of muscle.
