About the Payment Calculator

A fixed-rate loan is repaid with equal payments over a set term. This payment calculator finds that monthly amount from the loan size, rate and term, and shows how much goes to interest.

Use it to compare loan offers or decide what you can comfortably borrow.

How to Use the Payment Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount and loan term.
  2. Enter the interest rate and start date.
  3. Click Calculate to see the payment and schedule.

Payment Formula

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ) with r the monthly rate and n the number of months.

Example: A $20,000 loan at 6 percent for 5 years has a monthly payment of $386.66 and total interest of about $3,200.

How Term Length Changes the Payment

For a $25,000 loan at 7% APR, here is how the monthly payment and total interest change with the term:

TermMonthly paymentTotal interest
36 months$771.93$2,789
48 months$598.66$3,735
60 months$495.03$4,702
72 months$426.23$5,688

Stretching the term lowers the payment but increases total interest. That trade-off is the main thing the calculator helps you see.

Checking the Result by Hand

The monthly rate is the APR divided by 12. For 7% that is 0.5833%. Multiply it by the balance to get the first month's interest, which on $25,000 is about $145.83. Whatever remains of the payment reduces principal, so each month a little more goes to principal and a little less to interest.

When a Payment Calculator Is Not Enough

  • Loans with variable rates change the payment when the rate resets.
  • Interest-only periods and balloon payments do not follow the standard formula.
  • For a full month-by-month breakdown, use the amortization calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a monthly payment calculated?

With the amortization formula using the loan amount, rate and number of payments.

How can I lower my payment?

Borrow less, choose a longer term or find a lower rate, though a longer term raises total interest.

What is the difference between payment and total cost?

Total cost includes all interest paid over the loan.

Can I pay off early?

Usually yes. Check for prepayment penalties first.

How do I calculate a monthly payment without a calculator?

Use payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n). Divide the annual rate by 12 for r, and use the number of months for n. A spreadsheet PMT function does the same thing.

Why does a longer loan cost more?

Interest accrues on the remaining balance each month. A longer term keeps a larger balance outstanding for longer, so more interest builds up even though each payment is smaller.

Do extra payments lower my monthly payment?

Usually they shorten the loan instead. Extra principal reduces the balance, so you owe less interest each month and finish earlier, unless the lender recasts the loan.
