About the Calorie Calculator

Weight change comes down to energy balance: eat more calories than you burn and you gain weight, eat fewer and you lose it. The first step is knowing how many calories your body uses in a day.

This calorie calculator estimates your daily maintenance calories from your age, gender, height, weight and activity level, then shows targets for losing or gaining weight at different paces.

How to Use the Calorie Calculator

  1. Enter your age, gender, height and weight.
  2. Pick the activity level that best matches your normal week.
  3. Press Calculate to see maintenance calories and goal-based calorie targets.

How Daily Calories Are Estimated

The calculator first estimates BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiplies it by an activity factor: about 1.2 for sedentary, 1.375 for light activity, 1.55 for moderate, 1.725 for very active and 1.9 for extra active.

Example: A woman with a BMR of 1,370 who exercises moderately needs about 1,370 × 1.55 = 2,120 calories per day to maintain weight.

Calories for Weight Loss and Gain

About 3,500 calories equals roughly one pound (0.45 kg) of body fat, so a deficit of 500 calories a day leads to about one pound lost per week. Most guidelines suggest losing 0.5 to 1 kg per week and not eating below 1,200 calories (women) or 1,500 calories (men) without medical supervision.

Worked Example: Setting a Calorie Target

A 35-year-old woman who is 5'6" and 150 lb with light exercise needs about 1,914 calories a day to maintain her weight (Mifflin-St Jeor with a 1.375 activity factor). A 500-calorie daily deficit would put her near 1,414.

Rule of thumb: about 3,500 calories equals one pound of body fat, so a 500-calorie daily deficit points to roughly one pound per week. Real results vary, and the rate usually slows over time.

Safe Limits

Very low calorie intakes can cause nutrient gaps and muscle loss, and are best done under medical supervision. As a general guide, many calculators and clinicians avoid recommending targets below about 1,200 calories for women or 1,500 for men without supervision. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, adolescence and medical conditions change the numbers, so check with a clinician.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat a day?

It depends on your size, age, sex and activity. Many adult women need about 1,800 to 2,400 calories and many adult men about 2,200 to 3,000 to maintain weight.

How many calories should I cut to lose weight?

A moderate deficit of 300 to 500 calories below maintenance is a sustainable starting point for most people.

Do calories from different foods matter?

Total calories drive weight change, but protein, fiber and food quality affect hunger, muscle retention and health.

Why am I not losing weight at my target?

Tracking errors, water retention, changing activity and metabolic adaptation can all play a part. Reassess after two to three weeks.

How many calories should I eat a day to lose weight?

Start from your maintenance calories and subtract a moderate amount, commonly 250 to 500 per day. Avoid cutting too low, and check with a clinician if you have health conditions.

Do I need to eat back exercise calories?

Many activity multipliers already include typical exercise. If you add extra workouts beyond your chosen activity level, you may need slightly more food.

Why have I stopped losing weight?

As weight falls, maintenance calories fall too. Recalculate with your new weight and review your intake and activity.
