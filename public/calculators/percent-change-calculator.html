About the Percent Change Calculator

Percent change tells you how much a number has grown or shrunk compared with where it started, expressed as a percentage. It is used everywhere: a price that went up, a salary raise, a stock gain or loss, a drop in website traffic, a change in rent or a shift in a test score. This calculator does the math in both directions.

Use Find the percent change when you know the old and new values. Use Increase or decrease a number by a percent when you know the starting number and the percent, such as adding a 15% tip, applying a 20% discount or raising a budget by 8%. The results also show the absolute change, the multiplier and the percent difference.

Percent Change Formula

Percent Change = (New Value − Old Value) ÷ |Old Value| × 100

A positive result is a percent increase and a negative result is a percent decrease.

Example 1 (increase): A gallon of milk goes from $3.20 to $3.84. Change = 3.84 − 3.20 = 0.64. 0.64 ÷ 3.20 = 0.20, so the price rose 20%.

Example 2 (decrease): A stock falls from $150 to $120. Change = −30. −30 ÷ 150 = −0.20, so it dropped 20%.

Increasing or Decreasing by a Percent

Increase: New Value = Start × (1 + Percent ÷ 100)
Decrease: New Value = Start × (1 − Percent ÷ 100)

For example, a $200 item with a 15% increase becomes $200 × 1.15 = $230. A $200 item with a 15% discount becomes $200 × 0.85 = $170.

Percent Change vs. Percent Difference

The two are related but not the same. Percent change has a clear starting point: the old value is the reference. Percent difference compares two values when neither is the original, so it divides by their average.

Percent Difference = |A − B| ÷ ((|A| + |B|) ÷ 2) × 100

Use percent change for before-and-after situations. Use percent difference to compare two separate things, such as the prices of two different brands.

Common Pitfalls

  • Up then down is not break-even. A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease leaves you 25% below where you started (100 → 150 → 75). To undo a 25% drop you need a 33.3% gain.
  • Percentage points are not percent. If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rose by 1 percentage point, but the rate itself increased by 25%.
  • The old value is zero. Percent change is undefined because you cannot divide by zero. The calculator will tell you when this happens.
  • Negative starting values. The formula uses the absolute value of the old number so that a move toward a higher number is still shown as an increase.

Where Percent Change Is Used

  • Shopping: sale prices, markups, price inflation and unit cost changes.
  • Finance: investment returns, salary raises, growth in revenue and profit.
  • Science and school: change in measurements, population growth and grade improvement.
  • Business: year-over-year growth, conversion rate changes and traffic trends.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate percent change?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, then multiply by 100. For 80 to 100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase.

How do I calculate percent decrease?

Use the same formula. When the new value is lower than the old value, the result is negative, and that is the percent decrease. From 100 to 80: (80 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = −20%, a 20% decrease.

Why is a 20% drop not reversed by a 20% gain?

Because the percentages are based on different starting numbers. If $100 drops 20% it becomes $80, and 20% of $80 is only $16, which brings you to $96. You need a 25% gain to get back to $100.

What is the difference between percent change and percentage points?

Percentage points measure the plain subtraction of two percentages. Percent change measures the relative change. Going from 10% to 12% is 2 percentage points, or a 20% increase.

Can percent change be more than 100%?

Yes. If a value more than doubles, the increase is over 100%. Going from 50 to 150 is a 200% increase. A decrease cannot exceed 100% for positive numbers, because that would mean the value fell below zero.

How do I find the original number from a percent change?

Divide the new value by (1 + percent as a decimal). If a price after a 25% increase is $50, the original was 50 ÷ 1.25 = $40.
