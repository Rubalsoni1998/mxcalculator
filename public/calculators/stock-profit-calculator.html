About the Stock Profit Calculator

Your profit on a stock is what you receive when you sell, minus what you paid to buy, minus every fee along the way. This calculator does that math for any number of shares and adds the pieces investors often forget: commissions, dividends you collected while holding, how long you held, your annualized return, and the U.S. federal tax you may owe.

It also shows your break-even sell price and a what-if table, so you can see how a different exit price changes your result before you place the order.

How the Calculation Works

Total cost = (Shares × Buy price) + Buy fees
Net proceeds = (Shares × Sell price) − Sell fees
Capital gain = Net proceeds − Total cost
Total profit = Capital gain + Dividends received
Return (ROI) = Total profit ÷ Total cost × 100
Annualized return = (1 + ROI)365 ÷ days held − 1
Break-even price = (Total cost + Sell fees) ÷ Shares

For the tax estimate, a gain on shares held more than one year is long-term and taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on your total taxable income. A gain on shares held one year or less is short-term and taxed at your ordinary rate, from 10% to 37%. High earners may also owe the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax.

Example: You buy 100 shares at $50.00 and sell them 400 days later at $65.00, with no fees and no dividends. You are single with $80,000 of other taxable income.

Cost is $5,000, proceeds are $6,500, so the gain is $1,500, a 30.0% return. Annualized over 400 days that is about 27.0% a year. Because you held more than a year and your income is above the 0% zone ($49,450 for single filers in 2026), the gain is taxed at 15%: $225. Profit after federal tax is $1,275.

Key Terms

  • Cost basis: What you paid for the shares, including commissions. Selling costs reduce your proceeds.
  • Short-term vs. long-term: The line is one year. The holding period starts the day after you buy and includes the day you sell, so selling on the one-year anniversary is still short-term.
  • Break-even price: The sell price at which you recover your cost and fees with zero profit.
  • Annualized return: Your return restated as a yearly rate so you can compare a 3-month trade with a 3-year hold. It is only meaningful for holds of a few weeks or longer.
  • Wash sale: If you sell at a loss and buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, the IRS disallows the loss for now and adds it to the cost of the new shares.

Tips for Reporting and Reducing Tax

  • Hold past one year when it makes sense. The long-term rate is often much lower than your ordinary rate. Use the sell date in the calculator to compare.
  • Use losses. Capital losses first offset capital gains. If you still have a net loss, up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 if married filing separately) can reduce other income, and the rest carries forward.
  • Pick your lots. If you bought shares at different prices, choosing which lots to sell (specific identification) can change your gain. Check your broker's default method.
  • Keep records. Your broker reports sales on Form 1099-B, and you report them on Form 8949 and Schedule D.
  • Retirement accounts differ. Trades inside an IRA or 401(k) are not taxed when you sell, so set the tax estimate to No for those.

This estimate covers regular stock sales. It does not cover collectibles, employee stock options, qualified small business stock or foreign tax credits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate profit on a stock?

Multiply shares by your sell price and subtract any selling fees, then subtract what you paid (shares times buy price plus buying fees). Add any dividends you collected. A positive number is profit and a negative number is a loss.

How do I calculate the percentage return on a stock?

Divide your total profit by your total cost and multiply by 100. If you spent $5,000 and made $1,500, your return is 30%.

What is the break-even price on a stock?

It is the sell price at which you recover what you paid plus fees. With no fees it equals your buy price. With a buy fee of $10 on 100 shares bought at $50, the break-even price is $50.10.

How are stock profits taxed in the U.S.?

Gains on shares held more than one year are long-term and taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%. Gains on shares held one year or less are short-term and taxed as ordinary income at 10% to 37%. People with high income may also owe a 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax.

What is the long-term capital gains rate for 2026?

For 2026, a single filer pays 0% up to $49,450 of taxable income, 15% up to $545,500 and 20% above that. For married couples filing jointly the limits are $98,900 and $613,700.

Are dividends included in my stock profit?

This calculator adds the dividends you enter to your total profit and return. For taxes, it estimates only the capital gain from the sale. Qualified dividends are generally taxed at long-term rates and ordinary dividends at your regular rate, so you may owe tax on them separately.

Can I deduct a loss on a stock?

Yes. A capital loss first offsets capital gains. If your losses exceed your gains, up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 if married filing separately) can reduce other income, and the remaining loss carries forward to future years. The wash sale rule can delay a loss if you rebuy the stock within 30 days.

Do I pay tax if I reinvest the proceeds?

Yes. Selling a stock at a gain creates a taxable event even if you immediately buy something else. Only trades inside tax-advantaged accounts such as an IRA or 401(k) avoid tax at the time of the sale.
