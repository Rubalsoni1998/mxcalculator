About the Lottery Tax Calculator

Lottery winnings are taxed as ordinary income by the federal government and by most states. The lottery holds back part of your prize before you are paid, but that is only a down payment. This calculator estimates what you would keep from a Powerball, Mega Millions or other lottery prize after federal withholding, the extra federal tax you owe when you file, and state tax. It works for a lump sum or the 30-year annuity and can split the prize among several winners. Updated November 21, 2026.

How Federal Tax on Lottery Winnings Works

  • 24% withholding: The IRS requires lotteries to withhold 24% federal tax from winnings over $5,000.
  • Top rate of 37%: For 2026 the 37% bracket starts at taxable income above $640,600 for single filers and $768,700 for married couples filing jointly. A big jackpot lands almost entirely in that bracket.
  • The gap: A winner who owes 37% has only had 24% withheld, so another 13% is due when you file. Plan for it before you spend.
  • Progressive brackets: The first part of your income is taxed at lower rates, so the average rate on a mid-size prize is lower than the top rate.
  • No extra investment tax: Lottery prizes are not investment income, so the 3.8% net investment income tax does not apply.

Example: A $258.8 million cash prize, single filer with $80,000 of other taxable income, living in Texas.

The lottery withholds 24%, about $62.1 million. Because almost all of the prize is taxed at 37%, total federal tax is about $95.7 million, so roughly $33.6 million more is due at filing. There is no Texas income tax, so you keep about $163 million.

Lump Sum vs 30-Year Annuity

The lump sum is smaller than the advertised jackpot because the advertised figure assumes 30 annual payments. Nearly every big winner takes the cash. The annuity pays 30 payments over 29 years and each payment is 5% bigger than the last. Each year's payment is taxed in that year, and on a jackpot this size nearly every payment still lands in the top bracket. The annuity pays more in total (about $629 million before tax for the Mega Millions jackpot above, against $258.8 million in cash) but over 30 years. The right choice depends on how well you can invest and how you plan to spend. A tax professional and financial adviser can help before you claim.

State Tax on Lottery Winnings

State taxes range from nothing to more than 10%. California, Florida, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax lottery prizes. The highest rates are in New York (10.9% at the top rate, plus about 3.9% for New York City residents), the District of Columbia and New Jersey (10.75%), Oregon (9.9%), Minnesota (9.85%) and Maryland (8.95%).

Where a state withholds less than its top tax rate, you owe the rest when you file. Examples include New York (8.82% withheld), New Jersey (8% withheld), Oregon (8% withheld) and Minnesota (7.25% withheld). Of the 43 states that take part in multi-state lotteries, only Arizona and Maryland tax winners who live in another state.

Before You Claim a Big Prize

  • Sign the ticket and keep it safe, then talk to a tax professional before you claim.
  • Pool winners: If you split a prize with friends or co-workers, document the split so each person gets their own tax form and the claimant is not taxed on the whole prize.
  • Pay estimated tax: The extra federal tax due at filing may call for quarterly estimated payments to avoid penalties.
  • Gambling losses: You can only deduct losses up to your winnings, and only if you itemize.
  • Estate and gift tax: Giving away large amounts can trigger gift tax rules above the annual exclusion, so get advice first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax do you pay on lottery winnings?

Federal tax is 10% to 37% depending on your total income. The lottery withholds 24% from prizes over $5,000, and a large jackpot ends up taxed at 37%, so you owe the rest when you file. Most states add their own tax.

How much would I take home from a $629 million jackpot?

Take the cash value of about $258.8 million, subtract about 37% federal tax, then your state tax. In a state with no lottery tax you would keep roughly $163 million. In New York at the 10.9% top rate you would keep about $135 million before any city tax.

Which states do not tax lottery winnings?

California, Florida, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax lottery prizes. Alabama, Alaska, Hawaii, Nevada and Utah do not run a state lottery.

Is the lump sum or the annuity better?

The lump sum gives you the money now but it is a smaller amount. The annuity pays more in total over 30 years, but each payment is taxed when you get it and tax rates can change. Most winners take the lump sum.

Do I owe tax if I win in a different state?

Usually your home state taxes the prize, and most states do not tax non-residents. Arizona and Maryland are the exceptions. Some states do not tax prizes from their own lottery, so where you bought the ticket can matter.

Is the federal withholding of 24% enough?

Not for a large win. The top federal rate is 37%, so a big winner owes about 13% more at filing time. You may also need to make estimated tax payments.
