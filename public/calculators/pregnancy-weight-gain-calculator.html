About the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

Gaining the right amount of weight supports a healthy baby and a smoother recovery. The recommended amount depends mainly on your pre-pregnancy BMI and whether you are carrying one baby or twins.

This calculator estimates your target weight gain range and what is typical at your current week.

How to Use the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

  1. Enter your pre-pregnancy height and weight.
  2. Select the number of babies and your current week.
  3. Click Calculate to see your recommended range.

Recommended Total Gain (Singleton)

Based on U.S. Institute of Medicine guidelines: underweight (BMI under 18.5) 28 to 40 lb; normal weight 25 to 35 lb; overweight 15 to 25 lb; obese 11 to 20 lb. For twins the ranges are higher, for example 37 to 54 lb for normal weight.

Example: A woman with a pre-pregnancy BMI of 22 is advised to gain 25 to 35 lb, usually 1 to 4.5 lb in the first trimester and about 1 lb a week afterwards.

Where the Weight Goes

Baby about 7.5 lb, placenta 1.5 lb, amniotic fluid 2 lb, breast and uterus tissue 3 to 4 lb, increased blood and fluid 6 to 8 lb, and fat stores 6 to 8 lb.

Recommended Ranges

The Institute of Medicine guidelines, which ACOG uses, set total gain by pre-pregnancy BMI for a single baby:

Using the Numbers

  • Ranges are guidelines. Your provider may set a different goal for you.
  • Twin pregnancies have higher ranges; for normal-weight women the IOM range is 37 to 54 lb.
  • Eating for two does not mean double the calories; extra needs are modest and begin in the second trimester.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much weight should I gain in the first trimester?

Usually only 1 to 4.5 lb in total.

What if I gain too much or too little?

Talk to your provider. They can review diet, activity and growth scans.

Should I lose weight during pregnancy?

No. Do not diet. Aim for steady, healthy gain.

Do twins need more weight gain?

Yes. The recommended range is higher, about 37 to 54 lb for normal weight.

How much weight should I gain in pregnancy?

It depends on pre-pregnancy BMI. For a normal-weight person with one baby, the guideline is 25 to 35 pounds.

Where does the weight go?

It includes the baby, placenta, amniotic fluid, larger breasts and uterus, extra blood and fluid, and fat stores.

What if I gain too much or too little?

Talk to your provider. They can look at your health, your baby's growth and your nutrition rather than relying on the number alone.
