About the GFR Calculator

The glomerular filtration rate (GFR) measures how well your kidneys filter waste from the blood. Doctors use the estimated GFR (eGFR), calculated from a blood creatinine test, to detect and stage chronic kidney disease.

This calculator applies the CKD-EPI equation to your creatinine level, age and sex, and shows which kidney function stage the result falls in.

How to Use the GFR Calculator

  1. Enter your age and select your sex.
  2. Enter your serum creatinine value from a lab test and choose its unit.
  3. Click Calculate to see your eGFR and kidney function stage.

CKD-EPI Equation

The CKD-EPI creatinine equation estimates GFR in mL/min per 1.73 m² from creatinine, age and sex, using a curve that changes slope above and below a sex-specific creatinine threshold. It is currently the most widely recommended equation.

Example: A 50-year-old man with serum creatinine of 1.0 mg/dL has an eGFR of about 90 mL/min/1.73 m², which is in the normal range.

Worked Examples (CKD-EPI 2021)

For a 50-year-old with a serum creatinine of 1.0 mg/dL, the equation gives an eGFR of about 92 mL/min/1.73 m² for a man and about 69 for a woman. If creatinine were 1.6 mg/dL, a 50-year-old man would have an eGFR of about 52. The same creatinine means different eGFR values because muscle mass differs on average between sexes and across ages.

Reading the Result

Kidney function normally declines slowly with age. A single low eGFR does not by itself mean chronic kidney disease: doctors look for values that stay low for at least three months, along with urine tests for protein. Dehydration, medications, a very high-protein meal or intense exercise can temporarily change creatinine.

When eGFR May Mislead

  • Very high or low muscle mass changes creatinine independent of kidney function.
  • Some conditions and drugs change creatinine without affecting the kidneys.
  • Pregnancy and acute illness need clinical interpretation.

This tool does not diagnose anything. Discuss results with your clinician. Related sizing: body surface area.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a normal GFR?

A GFR of 90 or above is considered normal for adults, though it declines naturally with age.

Can I have kidney damage with a normal GFR?

Yes. Doctors also look at urine albumin and imaging. A single eGFR does not give a diagnosis.

What lowers GFR temporarily?

Dehydration, a recent heavy meat meal, some medications and intense exercise can influence creatinine and eGFR.

Should I worry if my eGFR is low?

Discuss it with a doctor. They will repeat the test and look for causes before deciding what to do.

What is a normal GFR?

A GFR of 90 or above is generally considered normal, though it declines with age. Your clinician interprets it with other tests.

What is the difference between GFR and eGFR?

GFR is the true filtration rate, which is hard to measure. eGFR is an estimate calculated from serum creatinine, age and sex.

Does the equation use race?

The 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation removed the race variable. This calculator uses age, sex and creatinine only.
