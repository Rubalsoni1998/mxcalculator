About the One Rep Max Calculator

Your one rep max is the heaviest weight you could lift for a single repetition with good form. Testing it directly is tiring and risky, so lifters estimate it from a set of several reps.

Enter the weight lifted and the number of reps you completed to estimate your 1RM and the weights for training at different percentages.

How to Use the One Rep Max Calculator

  1. Enter the weight you lifted.
  2. Enter the number of reps you completed.
  3. Click Calculate to see estimated 1RM and a percentage chart.

1RM Formulas

Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps). Estimates are most reliable for sets of 10 reps or fewer.

Example: If you bench 100 kg for 5 reps, Epley gives 100 × (1 + 5/30) = 117 kg and Brzycki gives 100 × 36 ÷ 32 = 112.5 kg.

Worked Example: 200 lb for 5 Reps

If you lift 200 lb for 5 clean reps, the Epley formula (weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)) gives an estimated one rep max of 233 lb. The Brzycki formula (weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)) gives 225 lb. Estimates differ by a few percent, and all are most reliable at 10 reps or fewer.

Safety

  • Estimating is safer than testing a true one-rep max, especially without a spotter.
  • Use sets you completed with good form; a grinding last rep skews the estimate.
  • Recalculate every few weeks as you get stronger.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a calculated 1RM accurate?

It is a good estimate, usually within 5 percent, if your set was taken close to failure with solid form.

Which formula should I use?

Epley and Brzycki are the most popular. Compare both and use the lower number for safe programming.

How often should I retest my max?

Every 8 to 12 weeks, or use the estimate from your working sets.

Is it safe to test a true 1RM?

Only with proper warm-up, safety bars or a spotter, and good technique.

Which one rep max formula is the most accurate?

No single formula is best for everyone. Epley and Brzycki are widely used and close to each other at low reps. Use the same formula consistently to track progress.

Is it safe to test a true 1RM?

It carries more risk than submaximal sets. Use a spotter or safety bars and warm up thoroughly, or estimate from a lighter set instead.

Can beginners use a 1RM calculator?

Yes, but form should come first. Estimates are less stable when technique is still changing.
