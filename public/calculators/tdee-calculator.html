About the TDEE Calculator

Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories you burn in a day, including resting metabolism, daily movement, exercise and digestion. Eating at your TDEE keeps your weight steady.

This TDEE calculator estimates your BMR, applies an activity multiplier and shows calorie targets for fat loss, maintenance and muscle gain.

How to Use the TDEE Calculator

  1. Enter your age, gender, height and weight.
  2. Select your activity level.
  3. Click Calculate to see your TDEE and goal calories.

TDEE Formula

TDEE = BMR × activity factor, with factors from 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9 (extra active). BMR is estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

Example: A person with a BMR of 1,700 who exercises moderately (factor 1.55) has a TDEE of 1,700 × 1.55 = about 2,635 calories. A deficit of 500 would be around 2,135.

Using Your TDEE

  • Lose fat: eat 10 to 20 percent below TDEE.
  • Maintain: eat at TDEE.
  • Build muscle: eat 5 to 15 percent above TDEE with enough protein.

Worked Example: 35-Year-Old Woman, 5'6", 150 lb

Using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, her basal metabolic rate is about 1,392 calories a day. Multiplying by a common activity factor gives her total daily energy expenditure (TDEE):

Activity levelMultiplierTDEE (calories)
Sedentary (little exercise)1.21,671
Lightly active (1-3 days/week)1.3751,914
Moderately active (3-5 days/week)1.552,158
Very active (6-7 days/week)1.7252,401

Eating at TDEE roughly maintains weight. Many people aiming to lose weight slowly start with a modest deficit of 10 to 20 percent below TDEE rather than a large cut.

Choosing an Honest Activity Level

Most people overestimate it. If you sit most of the day and work out three times a week, "lightly active" is usually closer than "moderately active". An estimate that is too high leads to eating more than you burn. Track weight and intake for two to three weeks and adjust the estimate to match what actually happens.

What TDEE Does Not Tell You

  • It is an estimate. Individual metabolism can differ from the equation by several hundred calories.
  • Weight change shifts TDEE, so recalculate as your weight changes.
  • It does not consider medical conditions or medications. Talk to a clinician or registered dietitian before major diet changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between TDEE and BMR?

BMR is resting calories only; TDEE adds all activity.

How accurate is TDEE?

It is an estimate. Track your weight for two to three weeks and adjust.

Should I choose the activity level carefully?

Yes. People often overestimate it. When unsure, pick the lower level.

How often should I recalculate?

When your weight or activity changes noticeably.

Is TDEE the same as calories needed to lose weight?

No. TDEE is the maintenance level. To lose weight you eat below it; to gain weight you eat above it.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

It gives a starting estimate, not a measurement. Compare it with your real weight trend over a few weeks and adjust by 100 to 200 calories if needed.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy your body uses at rest. TDEE adds the calories used for daily movement, exercise and digesting food.
