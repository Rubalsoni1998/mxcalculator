About the Percentage Calculator

Percentages are used everywhere: discounts, tips, grades, tax and statistics. A percentage is simply a number out of 100.

This calculator answers the most common percentage questions quickly.

Percentage Formulas

X% of Y = Y × X ÷ 100
Percent = part ÷ whole × 100
Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

Example: 15% of 80 is 12, and 12 is 15 percent of 80.

Common Percentage Problems, Solved

Reverse percentages

If a coat costs $60 after a 25 percent discount, the original price is not $60 plus 25 percent. The $60 is 75 percent of the original, so the original is 60 ÷ 0.75 = $80. Whenever you know the result after a change, divide by the remaining fraction.

Stacked discounts

A 20 percent discount followed by another 10 percent does not equal 30 percent off. The price becomes 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72 of the original, so the total discount is 28 percent. Multiply the remaining fractions; do not add the percentages.

Percent of a percent

Taking 50 percent of 20 percent is 0.50 × 0.20 = 10 percent. Convert each percentage to a decimal and multiply.

Percent Versus Percentage Points

If an interest rate rises from 4 percent to 5 percent, it rose by 1 percentage point, but by 25 percent in relative terms (1 ÷ 4). News reports and loan offers mix the two terms often, so check which one is meant. The percent change calculator handles the relative change directly.

Where People Use Percentages Every Day

  • Tips: 18 percent of a $45 bill is 0.18 × 45 = $8.10. A quick shortcut is to find 10 percent by moving the decimal point, then add half of that to get 15 percent.
  • Sales tax: Add the rate to the price, or see the sales tax calculator for backward calculations.
  • Grades: 42 correct out of 50 is 42 ÷ 50 = 84 percent. See the grade calculator for weighted scores.
  • Raises: A 3 percent raise on $52,000 adds $1,560 a year.

Common Mistakes

  • Going up and down by the same percent does not return you to the start. A 50 percent increase followed by a 50 percent decrease leaves you at 75 percent of the original value.
  • Using the wrong base. Percent change always divides by the old value, not the new one.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full precision through each step and round only the final answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100.

How do I take a percent off a price?

Multiply the price by (1 − discount percent ÷ 100).

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A move from 10% to 12% is two percentage points but a 20 percent increase.

How do I find the original price after a discount?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount).

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Convert the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100, then multiply by the number. For example, 15 percent of 80 is 0.15 times 80, which equals 12. You can also divide by 100 first and multiply by the percentage.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. If 18 out of 60 customers renewed, 18 divided by 60 is 0.30, so 30 percent renewed. The whole must be the number you are comparing against.

How do I add or subtract a percentage from a price?

To add 8 percent, multiply the price by 1.08. To subtract 15 percent, multiply by 0.85. This gives the final amount in one step, which is easier than calculating the percentage and then adding or subtracting.

Why can a percentage be greater than 100?

A percentage over 100 means the part is larger than the reference value. If sales go from $50 to $150, the new value is 300 percent of the old one, which is an increase of 200 percent.
