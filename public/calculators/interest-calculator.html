About the Interest Calculator

Interest is the cost of borrowing money or the reward for lending it. This calculator handles both simple interest, charged only on the original amount, and compound interest, charged on the balance including past interest.

Enter the principal, rate and time to see interest earned and the ending balance.

How to Use the Interest Calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount, interest rate and time.
  2. Select simple or compound interest and the compounding frequency.
  3. Click Calculate to see the interest and total.

Interest Formulas

Simple: I = P × r × t. Compound: A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t), with interest being A − P.

Example: $5,000 at 4 percent for 3 years earns $600 in simple interest, or $624.32 compounded annually.

Simple interest is earned only on the original amount. Compound interest is earned on the amount plus interest already added, so the gap widens the longer the money stays put.

Which One Applies to You?

  • Savings accounts, CDs and money market accounts usually compound daily or monthly.
  • Credit cards typically compound daily on unpaid balances.
  • Auto loans and many personal loans accrue interest on the remaining balance, which behaves like simple interest on each month's balance.
  • Some short-term notes and bonds use simple interest.

APR vs. APY

APR is the stated yearly rate. APY includes the effect of compounding, so it is higher than APR when interest compounds more than once a year. When comparing savings accounts, compare APY. For regular deposits, use the compound interest calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate simple interest?

Multiply principal by the annual rate and by the time in years.

Which is better for a saver, simple or compound?

Compound interest, since you earn interest on interest.

Which is better for a borrower?

Simple interest, because you pay less over time.

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is the stated yearly rate; APY includes the effect of compounding.

What is the formula for simple interest?

Interest = principal × rate × time. For $5,000 at 4% for 10 years, that is 5,000 × 0.04 × 10 = $2,000.

What is the formula for compound interest?

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t), where P is the principal, r the annual rate, n the compounding periods per year and t the years.

How often should interest compound for the best return?

More frequent compounding earns slightly more, but the difference between daily and monthly is small. The rate and the time matter far more.
