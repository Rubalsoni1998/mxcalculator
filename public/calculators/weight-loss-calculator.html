About the Weight Loss Calculator

Weight loss comes down to energy balance: eating fewer calories than your body burns. This calculator estimates how many calories you burn each day, subtracts a deficit that matches your weekly goal, and tells you the daily calorie target and how long it will take to reach your goal weight.

It also corrects a flaw in the simple "3,500 calories per pound" rule. As you lose weight your body burns fewer calories, so loss slows over time. The projection here recalculates your maintenance calories each week to give a more realistic timeline.

How the Calculation Works

BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor) = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women)
Maintenance calories = BMR × Activity multiplier
Daily deficit = Weekly goal (lb) × 3,500 ÷ 7
Calorie target = Maintenance − Deficit (never below 1,200 for women or 1,500 for men)
Simple time to goal = Pounds to lose ÷ (Deficit × 7 ÷ 3,500)

Example: A 35-year-old woman, 5 ft 6 in, weighs 190 lb, is lightly active and wants to reach 160 lb losing 1 lb per week.

Her BMR is about 1,574 calories and her maintenance is about 2,164. Subtracting 500 gives a target of about 1,664 calories a day. The simple estimate is 30 weeks. Because her maintenance drops as she slims down, the adjusted projection is about 38 weeks.

Safe Rates and Calorie Floors

The CDC and most medical groups describe 1 to 2 pounds per week as a safe, sustainable pace, which is a daily deficit of about 500 to 1,000 calories. People who lose weight gradually are more likely to keep it off. Harvard Health and the Cleveland Clinic advise that most women should not eat below about 1,200 calories a day and most men not below about 1,500 without medical supervision, because meeting nutrient needs becomes very difficult.

Key Terms

  • BMR: Basal metabolic rate, the calories your body burns at complete rest.
  • Maintenance calories (TDEE): Total daily energy expenditure, or BMR plus the calories from daily movement and exercise. Eating this amount keeps your weight stable.
  • Calorie deficit: Eating below maintenance so your body uses stored fat for energy.
  • Plateau: The point where your smaller body's maintenance equals your intake. Loss stops unless you eat less or move more.

Tips for Sustainable Weight Loss

  • Start with a modest deficit. It is easier to sustain and preserves muscle better than crash dieting.
  • Prioritize protein and fiber. They help you feel full on fewer calories.
  • Move more. Strength training protects muscle, and walking adds calories burned without much hunger.
  • Weigh in on a schedule. Weight bounces by several pounds with water, so look at the weekly or monthly trend.
  • Recalculate as you go. Update your weight every few weeks, since your target changes as you lose.
  • Get medical advice when it matters. Talk to a doctor first if you have a health condition, take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have a history of disordered eating.

If thinking about food or weight feels distressing or out of control, support is available from the National Alliance for Eating Disorders.

Results are estimates. Individual metabolism varies, and real-world weight loss is not perfectly linear.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Estimate your maintenance calories, then subtract about 500 for roughly a pound of loss per week. Do not go below about 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision.

How many calories do I need to cut to lose 1 pound a week?

About 500 per day. A pound of body fat holds roughly 3,500 calories, so a 500 calorie daily deficit adds up to about 3,500 over a week.

How fast can I safely lose weight?

The CDC recommends about 1 to 2 pounds per week. Faster loss raises the risk of muscle loss, nutrient shortfalls and regaining the weight.

Why does weight loss slow down over time?

A lighter body burns fewer calories, so the same intake produces a smaller deficit. The 3,500-calorie rule is only an approximation, which is why this calculator recalculates maintenance each week.

How long will it take to lose 30 pounds?

At 1 pound a week the simple answer is 30 weeks, and the adjusted projection is usually somewhat longer. At 2 pounds a week the simple answer is 15 weeks.

What is the minimum calories per day for weight loss?

Harvard Health and the Cleveland Clinic advise at least about 1,200 calories for most women and 1,500 for most men unless a health professional is supervising you.

What is a healthy goal weight?

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered the healthy range for most adults. For many people, losing 5 to 10% of body weight already improves blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol. A doctor can help you choose a goal that fits you.

Does exercise matter more than diet?

Both help. Food intake usually has the larger effect on the calorie deficit, while exercise builds muscle, improves health and helps keep the weight off. Enter your real activity level so your maintenance estimate is realistic.
