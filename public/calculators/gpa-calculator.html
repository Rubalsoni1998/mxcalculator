About the GPA Calculator

Your grade point average (GPA) is the average of your course grades weighted by credit hours. Colleges, scholarships and employers often use it to compare students.

Add your courses, grades and credits to find your GPA, or include prior credits to compute a cumulative GPA.

How to Use the GPA Calculator

  1. Enter each course's grade and credit hours.
  2. Add your previous GPA and credits if you want a cumulative result.
  3. Click Calculate to see your GPA.

GPA Formula

GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ credits. On a 4.0 scale A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.

Example: A (3 credits), B (4 credits) and A− (3 credits): (4.0×3 + 3.0×4 + 3.7×3) ÷ 10 = 35.1 ÷ 10 = 3.51.

Worked Example

Take four courses: an A (4.0) worth 3 credits, a B+ (3.3) worth 4, a B- (2.7) worth 3 and an A- (3.7) worth 2. Quality points are 12 + 13.2 + 8.1 + 7.4 = 40.7 over 12 credits, so the GPA is 3.39.

Details That Change Your GPA

  • Schools use different scales: some give no plus or minus, and A+ may be 4.0 or 4.3.
  • Weighted GPAs give extra points for honors or AP courses; a weighted GPA can exceed 4.0.
  • Repeated courses may replace or average with the old grade depending on policy.

Planning Ahead

To raise a cumulative GPA, the effect of a new semester depends on how many credits you already have. Check the grade you need with the grade calculator. For scholarship or graduation thresholds, confirm the rules with your school.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good GPA?

A GPA of 3.0 or higher is generally considered good, and 3.5 or above is strong.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Weighted GPAs give extra points for honors or AP courses; unweighted use the plain 4.0 scale.

How can I raise my GPA?

Retake low-grade courses where allowed and focus on high-credit classes.

Is GPA the same at every school?

No. Grading scales differ, so check your institution.

How do I calculate GPA?

Multiply each grade point by the course credits, add those up, and divide by total credits.

What is a good GPA?

It depends on the school and goals. Many scholarships and programs use thresholds such as 3.0 or 3.5; check each one.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Unweighted GPA ignores course difficulty and tops out at 4.0. Weighted GPA adds extra points for advanced courses.
