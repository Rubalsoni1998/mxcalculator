How macros are calculated

The calculator first estimates your resting energy use (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, multiplies it by your activity level to get maintenance calories, then adjusts that figure for your goal. The calorie target is split by the percentages of the diet style you choose, and each share is converted to grams.

Choosing a split

A balanced split suits most people. Higher protein can help you feel full while losing weight and supports muscle when you train. Lower-carb and ketogenic splits shift calories toward fat. Any split works if you can keep to it, so pick the one that fits your food preferences.

Treat the numbers as a starting point. Track your weight and energy for two to three weeks, then adjust calories up or down by about 5–10%.
