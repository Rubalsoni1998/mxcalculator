What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories your body uses in 24 hours. It has three main parts: your basal metabolic rate (calories burned at rest), the energy used digesting food, and the energy of movement and exercise.

Choosing a formula

Mifflin-St Jeor is the most widely recommended for the general population. The revised Harris-Benedict equation is an older alternative. Katch-McArdle uses lean mass, so it can be more accurate for people who know their body fat percentage, especially athletes.

Your TDEE changes as your weight and activity change, so recalculate every few weeks. About 3,500 calories equal one pound (7,700 for one kilogram) of body fat, which is how the weekly goals are set.
