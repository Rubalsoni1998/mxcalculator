How Auto Loans Work

An auto loan is a secured loan used to purchase a vehicle. The car itself acts as collateral. If you fail to make payments, the lender can repossess the vehicle. Auto loans typically have fixed interest rates and fixed monthly payments over a set term, usually ranging from 36 to 72 months (3 to 6 years).

Key Factors Affecting Your Payment

  • Auto Price: The negotiated purchase price of the vehicle.
  • Loan Term: The number of months you have to repay the loan. Longer terms lower your monthly payment but increase the total interest paid.
  • Interest Rate (APR): The cost of borrowing money. Rates depend on your credit score, the loan term, and whether the car is new or used.
  • Down Payment: Money paid upfront. A larger down payment reduces the loan amount and monthly payments.
  • Trade-In Value: The amount the dealer offers for your old vehicle. This acts like a down payment.
  • Sales Tax & Fees: Taxes and registration fees can add significantly to the total cost. In many states, sales tax is calculated on the difference between the new car price and the trade-in value.

Dealership vs. Direct Lending

Direct Lending: You get a loan directly from a bank, credit union, or online lender. You receive a check or pre-approval to pay for the car. This often allows you to shop around for the best rate before visiting a dealer.

Dealership Financing: The dealer arranges the loan for you through their partner lenders. While convenient, dealers may mark up the interest rate to make a profit. It's often wise to have a pre-approved offer from a bank to negotiate a better rate at the dealership.
