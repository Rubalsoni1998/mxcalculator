What is Inflation?

Inflation is the decline of purchasing power of a given currency over time. A quantitative estimate of the rate at which the decline in purchasing power occurs can be reflected in the increase of an average price level of a basket of selected goods and services in an economy over some period of time. The rise in the general level of prices, often expressed as a percentage, means that a unit of currency effectively buys less than it did in prior periods.

Forward vs Backward Calculation

Forward (Future Value): This calculates how much money you would need in the future to equal the purchasing power of a specific amount today. For example, if inflation is 3%, a $100 item today will cost $103 next year.

Backward (Historical Value): This calculates what a specific amount of money today would have been worth in the past. It effectively shows how much purchasing power has been lost.

Impact of Inflation

Inflation affects different people in different ways. It depends on whether they own assets (like property or stock) which tend to appreciate with inflation, or if they hold cash which loses value. Lenders are hurt by inflation because the money they get paid back buys less, while borrowers benefit because they pay back loans with money that is worth less.
